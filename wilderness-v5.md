# Wilderness v5: diversity, life and detail

> **Sequencing (2026-11-08):** the three releases below became layers across versions instead: the land (bioregions, tarn, glacier, Tinctures, gutter, terrain set-dressing) ships in **v4** with v4's own terrain work; the built things in **v5**; the people and small life in **v6**. See `wilderness-run.md`.

Austin, 2026-11-08, after walking v2.1. Runs after v4. Austin picked from `IDEAS-v5.md` on 2026-11-08 (below); the placement plan follows when v5's turn comes.

"If v4 is about improving high-level map nuance, v5 should be about more diversity, more little characters hiding out there, a few more humanoids in particular." Ideas are brainstormed in `IDEAS-v5.md`; Austin picks.

"More variation around the map. The same stones and plants occur everywhere. It would be more powerful if we had more distinct bioregions. The forest, the fens and the blue crystal area do this well. Need more sense that areas have personality: their own flora, fauna, even geology."

## The brief

- The region today has three places with a real character of their own: the Deodand Wood, Corpse-Candle Fen and the Lattice. Everything else shares one palette of grass, heather, rocks and fauna, so the moor between the landmarks reads as the same moor.
- v5 divides the bowl into named bioregions, each with its own ground cover and flowers, its own stone (colour, shape, how it lies: scree, pavement, tors, boulders), its own trees or absence of them, its own small life and sounds, and its own weather in the air (dust, seed, mist, ash). A visitor should know which region they are in with the HUD off, and crossing a boundary should feel like something.
- Candidates to grow from what exists: the orchard country round Medlar Close; the salt and grey reeds of the Wrong Colour spreading into a real salt-flat; the glacier's foreland and moraine; the high heath under the eastern wall; the river's canyon; the keep's home moor. Each gets a one-line character and a palette before anything is built.
- The rule from v2 holds: creatures tied to places. Each bioregion gets at least one thing that lives only there.
- **Light and illumination** (Austin): v4 raises the floor and adds a few local lights; v5 treats light as part of a place's character and as life: each bioregion's own quality of light and afterglow, lights that are tended or carried by someone, lights that move or answer, and the shadows they throw.

### The sun-gutter lasts longer

Austin, on reading the picks: "these are making me realise how many things depend on sun-guttering. The gutter may need to last a bit longer." Today (`sky.js`): a gutter runs 6.5 to 9 s in all, of which about three to five are at the dim floor, with 1.4 s down and 2.5 s back; they come every 70 to 150 s (the first at 25 to 55 s). v5 lengthens it: 12 to 16 s in all, about eight at the floor, every 60 to 110 s, so that the lamps, the beetles, the peafowl, the toadstone and the river's glow have time to be seen and the Leerie's row stays lit long enough to walk under. Everything keyed to the gutter reads `uFlicker` or the `gutter` event, so the one change reaches them all.

### Too much for one version

Austin: "really excellent stuff. I do think this might be too much for a single version?" It is, so v5 ships in three numbered releases, each curated and merged on its own, and the plan stops being a plan at whatever point the night ends:

1. **v5.0, the land:** the eight bioregions with their one creature each, the tarn's new shore, the glacier rebuilt, the Tinctures, the longer gutter, and the cheap set-dressing that belongs to a region (the Collation, the Discontinued Track, the Slipper, the Long Figure, the Surveyor's Pegs, the Unfinished Prospect if doable).
2. **v5.1, the people:** the nine humanoids, the Link-Boy and the Kept Fire.
3. **v5.2, the small life and the light:** the six little characters, the glyph motif, the Luminous Reach.

If Austin wakes between releases, what has shipped is the version and the rest is the next.

The shadow map is redrawn each frame of the jerk while the walker is within 220 m of the line. The update is registered with `{ pos: midpoint, r: 115, near: 600, hz: 4 }`, so from anywhere in the bowl it runs at frame rate.
- **The groan:** `hawser-groan` in `src/audio.js`, at the chain's midpoint (−79.9, 49.6, −284.1). Three low saws at 38, 57 and 76.5 Hz sag in pitch and catch (stick-slip in their gain) over a brown grind. The settle's thud comes at 0.42 s. Then the iron rings out on four inharmonic partials (113–747 Hz) over 2–5 s. Its inverse falloff runs from a 160 m reference with a 3 km range and heavy reverb, so it is still a third of its level 500 m off and the whole bowl hears it. `tools/audio-test.mjs` fires it 200 m off (one more firing, so 42 checks).
- **The dust:** one `Points` draw, only while it falls (6.5 s). It has 2600 motes at high and 1100 at low along the whole line, shaken outward, then falling and drifting downwind. The shiver runs up the line from the paving in half a second. Each mote is gone in 4–6 s and is faded near the eye.
- **Cost at high:** 3 draw calls in the main pass (links, anchor, figure), +1 (the dust) during a shift, and +2 in the shadow pass near it. 16.6k triangles (links 16.0k, anchor 0.5k, figure 0.2k), 10.2k at low. ### AP. The Battlemented Ridge (`src/v4/mountains.js`, a marked block; `?ridge=0` leaves the crest as it was)

**Idea:** the north-east crest stops being rock and becomes a wall: a run of squared merlons with embrasures between, too regular to be natural, going on until the haze takes the count.

- **Bearings and count:** the run is bearings 9-57 with the full-height merlons on 12-54, seven merlons (centres 10.2, 17.8, 25.4, 33.0, 40.6, 48.2, 55.8). The brief gives 20-45, which at r about 470 is only 205 m and three merlons at 34 m wide, too few for "repeat until fog takes the count", so the run is widened to the clear ground below the Vigilant's 60. Bearings 58-100 are bit-identical (grid-checked every 0.25 degree and 4 m, ridge on against off: no change outside 9-57).
- **The wave:** a square wave in bearing, period 7.6 degrees (62 m at r 470): merlon 0.54 of it (4.1 degrees, 34 m), embrasure 28 m. Each merlon has a flat top at the natural crest height (`T_CH`) at its centre plus 30 m, less 0-2 m of wear (a hash per merlon), plus 0.5 m of noise on the top; merlons 2 and 5 are broken to 15 m. The two end merlons (centres 10.2 and 55.8) ride a 3-degree envelope (about a third of full height) so the run does not begin with a cliff.
- **How it composes:** `applyMountains` raises the profile, after bedding, talus and the apron so the 11 m ledges do not eat the tops: inside a merlon's bearing interval and a 14 m deep radial window (crest r - 10 to + 4, nothing behind it) `m = lerp(m, max(m, top), mask)`. Raising was chosen over lowering the notches: the natural crest's ridged pinnacles (up to 52 m) would stand above a lowered "wall" and break the square skyline, so the pinnacle term is also damped by 85% in the top 15% of the face inside the run, which makes the embrasure floor a plain crest and the merlons the only things standing. `crestAt` reads the heightfield, so it reports the new skyline: e.g. at bearing 33 the crest goes from y 179 to 211, at 42 from 188 to 240 (a merlon over a low natural crest), and in the embrasure at 28 stays at 159 to 165. The crest here is y 160-210 on the ground (not the 300 the plan gives), so the merlons stand 30-50 m over the embrasure floor.
- **Resolution:** the mesh is 1000 m over 480 segments at high (2.08 m a sample) and 320 at low (3.1 m). A merlon's edge rises over about 4 m (0.5 degree, two samples at high, one and a bit at low), so the steepest face is about 82 degrees at high (30 m over 4.2 m) and 84 at low, not vertical but vertical-looking from the floor; its flat top and the shadowed face give the line. The back face drops 14 m behind in under 8 m.
- **The Vigilant:** `crestAt(72)` is r 476, y 183.9 with the ridge on and off. In `v5-06-ridge-vigilant-after-v2.png` (from the Outwash, looking east) the ridge's east end stands to the left and the colossus to the right of centre with natural crest between: they do not read as one.
