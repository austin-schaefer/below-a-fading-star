# Scale and awe: ideas for wilderness v4

Brainstormed 2026-11-07 for the v4 scale-and-awe pass (`wilderness-v4.md`), after Austin's note that the vibe works but the "woah, epic, ancient wonders" feeling does not always land. The Great Span and its two sealed gates are already specced and are not repeated here. Letters continue from `IDEAS.md`. Names are **[Proposed]** placeholders. Coordinates are metres (x east, z south); the walkable floor ends at r 300 and the walls reach full height at r 440. The sun is fixed low in the west-south-west. Costs: **cheap** under a day, **medium** a few days, **big** a week or more. Austin picks; nothing here is built until he does.

## On the walls

- **AI. The Frontispiece** [Proposed]. A rock-cut façade 200 m tall carved into the north wall: pilasters whose capitals go up into the haze, and a row of doorways at heights no stair reaches, the lowest 40 m up. While the sun gutters, the deepest doorway gives off a slow breath of pale mist. *Lovecraft, The Nameless City (doors made for other bodies); John Martin's rock-cut cities.* Where: north wall at about (−120, −400), facing the empty quarter between the Armiger's Tower and the Lattice. **Big**, terrain (a flat cut seated in the wall).
- **AJ. The Ashlar Unparted** [Proposed]. Part of the north-east wall is a quarry, not a mountain: stepped benches cut into the rock, and one block bigger than Carcanet Keep, dressed on five faces and still joined to the cliff along the sixth. Wedge slots run along that seam, and some still have their wedges in. *Baalbek's unmoved monoliths; At the Mountains of Madness, where the range itself turns out to be worked.* Where: north-east wall at about (230, −330), above the Fosse (BF). **Medium**, terrain (terraced benches).
- **AK. The Annals** [Proposed]. A frieze runs round the bowl at about 120 m up, a procession of figures each 40 m tall, worn almost smooth. It only reads in raking light: when the sun gutters, the shadows shift and for a few seconds the procession seems to be walking the other way. Too worn to say what it shows. *At the Mountains of Madness (the history in bas-relief); The Nameless City's frescoes.* Where: clearest on the east wall, which faces the sun; a band in the terrain shader all the way round. **Medium**, no geometry, shader only.
- **AL. The Galleries of Durance** [Proposed]. North of the sun's saddle, the west wall carries galleries, gantries and catwalks bridging clefts in the rock, with chains hundreds of metres long hanging from them. The sun is behind it all, so the walker sees only black lattice against the glow, and a chain swaying very slowly where no wind reaches. *Piranesi, Carceri d'invenzione; Hodgson's Night Land redoubt.* Where: west wall at about (−390, −80), above and behind the Armiger's Tower. **Medium**, no terrain (geometry set into the rock).
- **AM. The Acephalous Thrones** [Proposed]. The sun's saddle (which v4 widens) is flanked by two seated colossi carved from the wall, each 150 m tall, hands on knees, both headless, the breaks weathered as smooth as the rest. Against the sun they are pure silhouette, and the sun sits between them like an offering. When it gutters, their shoulders catch a thin rim of light, then lose it. *Abu Simbel by way of John Martin; Smith's Zothique, where the gods' images outlast the gods.* Where: either side of the saddle, about (−420, 100) and (−400, 200). **Big**, terrain (the saddle's two flanks recarved).
- **AN. The Dry Cataracts** [Proposed]. North of the Tumbling Water's fall, the east wall holds three spillway mouths 50 m high, cut square, with dressed aprons below and stained the colour of a sea long gone. Nothing comes out of them now. At each gutter a thin skein of dust pours from the middle one and settles before reaching the floor. *Clark Ashton Smith's dead seas; Hubert Robert's dry fountains.* Where: east wall at about (390, 120), across the floor from the Gnomon (AX). **Medium**, terrain (mouths and aprons).
- **AO. The Escarpment Lamps** [Proposed]. High on the north-west wall, in the part the sun never reaches, a scatter of hundreds of tiny warm window-lights, far too high and too many for anything on the floor. Each time the sun gutters, one of them goes out and stays out. Over a long walk the wall gets darker. *Hodgson, The Night Land (the lit windows of the Last Redoubt); Vance's sense of a world being shut down room by room.* Where: north-west wall, about (−300, −330) and upward. **Cheap**, no terrain (points in a shader).
- **AP. The Battlemented Ridge** [Proposed]. Along part of the north-east crest the ridgeline is too regular: merlons and embrasures each 30 m wide, some fallen, so the skyline reads as a rampart that is the mountain. Only visible against the sky; up close it would just be rock. *At the Mountains of Madness (the cubes and ramparts on the peaks).* Where: north-east crest, between the Lattice's and the Gradine's lines of sight. **Cheap**, terrain (quantised ridge noise in a marked block).

## In the air and sky

- **AQ. The Cincture** [Proposed]. A thin, broken arc crosses the sky from the south-east horizon to the north, a band of something built around the world long ago. The sun lights it red along its western edge, and there are gaps where whole lengths have fallen. During a gutter it is the brightest thing in the sky. *Wolfe's Urth, where the sky is full of old works; Vance's last-age skies.* Where: the sky dome, arching over the Lattice. **Cheap**, no terrain (sky shader).
- **AR. The Occultation** [Proposed]. Every several minutes a band of shadow 400 m long sweeps across the floor from west to east at walking pace, as if something vast were passing between the bowl and the sun. Look up and there is only haze, and the sun's disc dims along one edge. Birds go quiet under it. *Burke on obscurity and power; Hodgson's watchers too large to see whole.* Where: the whole floor; it runs from the sun's saddle to the east wall. **Medium**, no terrain (an invisible shadow caster or a moving light cookie).
- **AS. The Ultramontane Spire** [Proposed]. Over the north rim, beyond the mountains, stands a single spire so far off that the fog leaves it almost the colour of the sky, yet it is taller than the peaks in front of it. It never changes. From most places it is hidden; from three or four spots it shows in a notch. *At the Mountains of Madness (the greater range beyond the range); Dunsany's distant cities.* Where: beyond the north wall, past the Frontispiece, seen best from the Sunken Court and the Lattice's south edge. **Cheap**, no terrain (one far billboard or low-poly mesh in the fog).
- **AT. The Tresses** [Proposed]. Only during a gutter: a prominence lifts off the sun's limb, an arch of dark red flame larger than the sun itself, which slowly falls back as the light recovers. Each gutter it leaves from a different place on the rim. *Hodgson's dying sun; Vance, the sun that "might go out at any moment".* Where: the sun's disc. **Cheap**, no terrain (sky shader, tied to `sky.flicker`).
- **AU. The Becalmed Hull** [Proposed]. A hull the size of the keep hangs motionless 250 m above the north-east quarter, keel down, ribs showing through gaps in its plating, held up by nothing anyone remembers. Over an hour it turns a quarter-circle. Its shadow is a ragged dark shape high on the east wall that nobody can account for until they look up. *Wolfe's fliers and Vance's air-cars as late, failing craft; Smith's sky-borne relics.* Where: above about (180, −200), shadow on the east wall. **Medium**, no terrain.

## Across the floor

- **AV. The Toppled Order** [Proposed]. A column fell here, and its drums lie in a line 110 m long where they rolled apart, each drum 12 m tall and fluted, the capital at the end tilted on its side like a house. The walker passes between drums as between buildings. The base still stands, a stump 20 m across with nothing near it that it could have held up. *Selinunte's Temple G; Hubert Robert's fallen orders.* Where: running north from about (−215, −190) toward (−235, −300), between the Armiger's Tower and the Tumulus (AY). **Medium**, no terrain.
- **AW. The Plinth Road** [Proposed]. A processional way of paired plinths, each 20 m tall, runs north from Medlar Close toward the wall. On top of each, only feet remain, and they are not all the same kind of feet. The road ends at the lip of the Fosse (BF), facing sheer rock, as if it once went on into the mountain. *John Martin's processional avenues; The Nameless City's proportions.* Where: from about (130, −150) to (165, −290). **Medium**, no terrain.
- **AX. The Gnomon** [Proposed]. A leaning needle 120 m tall, with hour-lines inlaid in a fan across the moor around it. The sun no longer moves, so the shadow lies along the same line forever: 500 m of it, across the floor and up the east wall. Every hour-line except that one is overgrown. When the sun gutters, the shadow fades out, and for those seconds the dial reads no hour at all. *Vance's sun that has stopped keeping time; Wolfe's late, useless instruments.* Where: about (205, 120), between the Arcature and the river; the shadow runs east-north-east. **Medium**, no terrain.
- **AY. The Tiered Tumulus** [Proposed]. The rise in the north-west quarter turns out, as you climb it, to be terraced: each tier 8 m high, the risers overgrown to look like slope from below. Only near the top do the corners come clear and the walker understands they have been climbing a building the whole time. *Hodgson's Last Redoubt; Mesopotamian mounds read as hills.* Where: centred about (−160, −240), r 45. **Medium**, terrain (terracing the heightfield in a marked block).
- **AZ. The Houseless Portal** [Proposed]. A doorframe 60 m tall stands alone on the moor with no walls and nothing behind it. Through it the view is the same land, but older: the haze thicker, the grass greyer. When the sun gutters outside, the sun seen through the door does not. *Dunsany's doors to elsewhere; Hubert Robert's arches framing more ruin.* Where: about (225, −240), between the Plinth Road and the Gradine. **Medium**, no terrain (a render-to-texture or a regraded view through the frame).
- **BA. The Hawser** [Proposed]. A chain whose links are each 15 m long runs taut from the Frontispiece's highest doorway, down across the north-west floor, and into the paving of the Sunken Court, where it disappears into the ground. Once in a long while it shifts one link, with a groan the whole bowl hears and a shiver of dust down its length. *Piranesi's chains; Hodgson's sense of something held down.* Where: from about (−120, −380) at height down to the court (−40, −165). **Medium**, no terrain.

## Underfoot and beneath

- **BB. The Puteal** [Proposed]. At the crown of the Tiered Tumulus is a round shaft 30 m across, with dressed sides and a spiral stair wide enough to be a road winding down into dark. Far below, a small red disc: the sun reflected in water hundreds of metres down. Each gutter reaches the reflection a beat late. *Burke: depth is more sublime than height; Piranesi's wells; The Night Land's underground fields.* Where: atop AY at about (−160, −240). **Big**, terrain (needs a hole in the heightfield and a mesh shaft).
- **BC. The Spiracles** [Proposed]. Bronze gratings 10 m across, set flush in the moor near the Arcature, "built to think". Through the bars you see vaulted halls far below lit a dull red, and warm air rises with a very slow mechanical sound. Each time the sun gutters, the light below brightens, as if drawing on it. *The Nameless City's lit depths; Forster's buried machine, by way of Vance.* Where: three or four grates within 40 m of the Arcature (160, 60). **Medium**, terrain (shallow seats).
- **BD. The Foundered Nave** [Proposed]. A sinkhole where the moor has fallen through a roof. Looking over the edge, the walker is level with the capitals of a buried hall whose columns go down out of sight, with rubble and grass slumped across its aisles. *The Nameless City; Hubert Robert's half-buried galleries.* Where: about (255, 40), between the Arcature and the east wall. **Big**, terrain (a pit carved in a marked block, a walled mesh below).
- **BE. The Stair to the Lees** [Proposed]. A flight of steps as broad as the causeway runs down from the tarn's north shore into the water. The first steps are clear, then they blur, then they are gone into black. When the sun gutters, the steps below seem to continue a little further. *Smith and Dunsany's stairs into water; Vance's drowned cities.* Where: Lees Tarn's north shore, about (−95, 55), visible from the Sward. **Cheap**, terrain (a short cut in the shore).
- **BF. The Fosse** [Proposed]. A straight-cut channel 40 m wide and 30 m deep, with dressed walls, runs in a long arc along the foot of the north-east wall from the Gradine toward the Lattice. Mist lies in it and drifts slowly uphill. The Plinth Road ends at its lip; the Gradine's first step rises from its far bank. *Babylonian moats in John Martin's canvases; At the Mountains of Madness's carved tunnels and channels.* Where: arc from about (282, −172) to (110, −290). **Big**, terrain (carving the channel).

## Scale tricks

- **BG. The Postern** [Proposed]. Set into the foot of the Span's sealed west gate, the Frontispiece and the Ashlar Unparted: a door of human size, open or ajar, plainly made later and smaller by someone else. One familiar measure, and everything above it jumps in size. *Burke on comparison; Hubert Robert's tiny figures in ruins.* Where: at the foot of each giant work. **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **BH. The Late Climbers** [Proposed]. Three or four dark specks on the Gradine's highest visible steps and on the Span's eastern stair, each moving up one step a minute and never seen to arrive. From the floor they are barely a pixel, which is the point. *John Martin's minute figures; Hodgson's Silent Ones.* Where: the Gradine and the Span's stairs. **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **BI. The Recession of Ranges** [Proposed]. Break the bowl's walls into distinct depth tiers, each a step paler and bluer-violet in the fog, the farthest ridge nearly the colour of the sky, so the walls read as range behind range rather than one wall. *Leonardo's and Martin's aerial perspective; Dunsany's "farther than the farthest hills".* Where: all the walls. **Cheap**, no terrain (fog by tier, in `atmosphere.js`).
- **BJ. The Wrack Line** [Proposed]. A thin, broken deck of cloud lies at about 70 m. The Span's deck, the mother crystal, the raised columns of the Sunken Court and the Arcature's top all break through it, and it slides slowly across them. Cloud at a known height measures everything that pierces it. *Martin's cloud-wrapped towers; Burke's vastness.* Where: over the middle of the bowl. **Medium**, no terrain.
- **BK. The Long Answer** [Proposed]. Sound obeys the bowl's size: one deep note sounds from somewhere beyond the north wall once per gutter, and its echo comes back off each wall in turn, 1 s, 2 s and 3.5 s later. Footsteps on the Span's deck return a faint slap from the far pier. *Burke on sudden great sounds; the cathedral-scale acoustics of Piranesi's prisons.* Where: global, delays computed from the walls' distances. **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **BL. The Diminishing Treads** [Proposed]. Forced perspective on every long ascent: the Gradine's, the Span stairs' and the Frontispiece's courses shrink slightly as they rise, and the block joints get finer, so the eye reads far more height than is built. *Baroque stage scenery; Borromini's false gallery.* Where: the Gradine, the Span's stairs, AI and AJ. **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **BM. The Rook-Wheel** [Proposed]. Rooks wheel at mid-height round the mother crystal, the Span's piers and the keep's donjon: a known bird, the size the walker expects, turning against a vast surface. They go up with the thermals and scatter when the sun gutters. *Hubert Robert's birds over ruins; Martin's scale-giving flocks.* Where: the tallest three things in the bowl. **Cheap**, no terrain (extends the crows in `fauna/`).
- **BN. The Held Breath** [Proposed]. Stand still and look up at something vast for a few seconds and the bowl answers: the wind falls, near grass stops moving, the drone drops a fifth, the grade deepens a touch. Move and it all comes back. This is the response to looking, not to the sun's guttering (W). *Burke: astonishment is the soul's motions suspended.* Where: anywhere, keyed to pitch above the horizon and a large object in view. **Cheap**, no terrain.

## Rules of awe

1. **Never show the whole.** Let fog, glare or the mountain cut off the top, the end or the far side; the imagination finishes it bigger (Burke on obscurity).
2. **Repeat one element until the fog takes the count.** Piers, drums, steps, windows, merlons at even intervals are Burke's artificial infinite; variety makes things look smaller.
3. **Every vast thing needs a measure.** A door, a tread, a lamp, a rook, a speck of a figure beside it, or the eye reads it as a model (Martin, Hubert Robert).
4. **Silhouette against the glow beats detail.** The sun is fixed and low, so put the biggest shapes between the walker and the west, or let it catch them on one edge; texture is for the last 20 m.
5. **Built by others, for other bodies.** Proportions slightly wrong for us: risers chest-high, doors with no handles at heights no stair reaches; better builders, and not us (The Nameless City, Mountains of Madness).
6. **Stopped mid-work says more than ruined.** A block cut but not moved, a road ending at rock, a dial with one hour left: the age ended in the middle of a sentence (Vance, Wolfe).
7. **Vast things move slowly or once.** One link, one lamp, one step, one quarter-turn an hour; never animate the big things quickly, so the walker has to wait to see them change (Hodgson).
