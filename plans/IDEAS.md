# Enrichment ideas for the wilderness scene

Brainstormed 2026-11-07 from pre-1960s science fantasy and weird fiction. Names are **[Proposed]** placeholders in the game's register. Costs: **S** under a day, **M** a few days, **L** a week or more. ⟳ marks ideas that react to the player. Austin picks; nothing here is built until he does.

## Growing things

- **A. Heliotrope Sward.** A slope of pale, long-stemmed flowers whose heads all face the swollen sun. When the sun gutters they close together with a dry rustle crossing the moor, and reopen one by one as the light returns. *Vance, Dying Earth: every living thing depends on a failing sun.* **S**
- **B. Mandragore.** Among the boundary stones, pale forked roots lie half out of the soil, shaped a little too much like curled figures. ⟳ As you approach, a thin keening rises from below hearing and stops the moment you stop. *C. A. Smith, "The Mandrakes".* **S**
- **C. Foxfire.** The oldest trunks in the Deodand Wood carry a cold phosphorescence in their rotten bark, visible only after 20–30 s of standing still in the shade. *Lovecraft, Dream-Quest: the Enchanted Wood's fungous light.* **S–M**
- **D. Manikin Blooms.** A walled garden off the taverna holds waist-high flowers with small pale faces in their cups. ⟳ They turn to follow you; step close and a few make a tiny wet sound like a child about to cry. *Vance, "Mazirian the Magician".* **M**

## Inhabitants, seen and half-seen

- **E. The Vigilant.** On the boundary mountains sits a crouched shape the size of a hill, hard to tell from rock. Its head is turned a few degrees differently each time you look back from far across the region. It never does anything else. *Hodgson, The Night Land: the Watchers.* **M**
- **F. The Palmer.** A tall robed figure walks the causeway toward the tower so slowly you only notice by checking it against a paving stone a minute later. It never arrives and never turns its head. *Hodgson, The Night Land: the Silent Ones.* **S**
- **G. Salt-Pedlars.** Finger-high riders astride dragonflies over the fen. They hover at a polite distance, dart off, and sometimes leave a glint of white on a stone where they paused. *Vance, Dying Earth: the dragonfly riders who trade for salt.* **M**
- **H. The Wimpled Guest.** A cowled woman sits by the taverna's well; her wimple stirs as if something under it is shifting. ⟳ Look long and a warm heartbeat rises while the screen edges flush red and drowsy; look away and it ebbs. *C. L. Moore, "Shambleau".* **M**
- **I. The Sable Galley.** Every few minutes a sailless black galley crosses Lees Tarn, oars dipping in unison with no splash, a muffled drum under the wind. Never seen to land. *Lovecraft, Dream-Quest: the black galleys.* **M**
- **J. The Observance.** Once in a long cycle, hooded figures carry an empty litter from the keep gate to the Cardinal Stones, bow in the order Pride, Sloth, Avarice, Spite, and walk back. Nobody remembers why. *Peake, Titus Groan.* **M**
- **K. The Peripatetic Hut.** ⟳ A crooked hut on spindly stilts on the heath. Each time you look away it is at a different one of a handful of fixed spots, always facing you. *Leiber, Lankhmar: the walking hut.* **S–M**

## Ruins and the works of old hands

- **L. The Hypogeum.** A sunken tomb with a bronze door and a lintel of glyphs. Each time the sun gutters the glyphs shift one place along, as if reading themselves aloud. *C. A. Smith, "The Last Hieroglyph".* **S–M**
- **M. The Brink House.** A lightless manor on the lip of a chasm at the region's edge. Its broken windows show a different sky: a sun wheeling fast through days and nights, centuries running past inside. *Hodgson, The House on the Borderland.* **L**
- **N. The Gradine.** Steps cut up the face of the boundary mountains, each riser chest-high, climbing into haze past where you can follow. *Lovecraft, Dream-Quest: the steps of the gods.* **S**
- **O. The Mouldered Scriptorium.** A ring of stone lecterns under a roofless apse. Most books have gone to leaf-mould; a few pages still lift in the wind, and one book is held open by a stone someone placed. *Vance, Turjan's library; Peake's library.* **S**
- **P. The Careened Barque.** A tilted ship's skeleton, ribs and figurehead, beached on dry moor in a cracked basin, rust-coloured grass filling the hull. *Brackett, The Sword of Rhiannon: dead sea-bottoms.* **M**
- **Q. The Cutting.** A slumped stream bank shows strata: dressed stone, black glass, corroded metal lattice, stone again. Cities on cities; a carved hand juts from the third layer. *Wolfe (late echo): Urth's buried ages.* **S–M**

## Sky, light and sound

- **R. The Parhelion City.** Only while the sun gutters, a faint city of spires shows against it on the western horizon, pale gold and rose, like a sun-dog. Gone when the light recovers. *Dunsany, "Carcassonne"; Lovecraft's sunset city.* **S**
- **S. The Rust Wind.** Every several minutes a slow wall of red-brown haze rolls in from the west: the sun shrinks to a dull coin, fog turns ochre, the wind climbs, then it passes. *Brackett, the Mars stories.* **M**
- **T. The Speculum.** ⟳ Lees Tarn's reflection shows the sky as it was: a small white-yellow sun, a blue horizon. Your own reflection is not there. *Vance's elegy; Merritt, The Moon Pool.* **M**
- **U. The Laggard Shadow.** ⟳ Your shadow follows a half-beat late, and during a gutter its outline is briefly wrong: a hunch, an extra limb. *Moore and Lovecraft's uncanny double.* **M**
- **V. The Wrong Colour.** In one fen pool a light pulses at the bottom, flipping between complementary hues too fast to settle on; the reeds for a few metres around are grey and brittle. *Lovecraft, "The Colour Out of Space".* **S–M**
- **W. The Hush.** While the sun gutters every creature sound stops; only the wind remains, then everything resumes together, slightly too loud. *Hodgson and Vance: a world holding its breath.* **S**
- **X. The Undercroft Choir.** Inside the Sunken Court you hear wordless voices far below the paving, phasing in and out, silent the moment you step over the rim. *Merritt, The Moon Pool; Smith's subterranean rites.* **S**

## Things that answer the player

- **Y. The Settle.** ⟳ A stone bench on a rise facing the sun. Stand beside it half a minute and someone is sitting at its other end, only ever in the edge of vision; turn to look and it is empty. *Dunsany; Smith's Averoigne.* **S**
- **Z. The Carillon Flags.** ⟳ One stretch of causeway is paved in bronze-capped stones that each ring a different note underfoot. Walked at an even pace they play the same descending phrase; walked otherwise, they jangle. *Dunsany's bells; Vance's purposeless craft.* **S**
- **AA. The Susurrant Chamber.** ⟳ A roofless round room beside the keep. The longer you stand in it, the louder its walls hiss and whistle; at the loudest the plaster seems to pucker into lips. *Hodgson, "The Whistling Room".* **M**

## The game's own fiction

- **AB. The Menology.** A spiral of thirty-six low menhirs, one for each month of a run, the first carved with a March sign. ⟳ Each you touch warms faintly. The last few are cracked and unfinished, as if no one ever reached them. *Tied to the three-year run.* **S–M**
- **AC. The Ten Cathedrae.** Ten tall stone chairs in a ring on a windy height, one per sorcerer, inlaid in the colours of its two elements. All empty but one, where a faint figure sits slumped; which one changes between visits. *Smith's decadent courts; the ten sorcerers.* **M** (element pairs per sorcerer need confirming)
- **AD. The Vitrescence.** Near the Lattice, heather and grass grow faceted and glassy at the tips; a crow on a stone has one wing of crystal. ⟳ Stand still near the glacier too long and a ring of grass at your feet turns to biomesh. *F9AC's lore; Merritt, The Metal Monster.* **M**
- **AE. Nepenthe Hollow.** In a hollow of pale poppy-like flowers your stride slackens, sound dims, the grade goes warm and the wind falls away. Leaving feels like waking. *Dunsany, "Idle Days on the Yann"; Sloth's comfort.* **M**
- **AF. The Cucurbit.** In a side chamber of the keep, a cracked alchemical vat on a plinth. Inside floats a half-made translucent figure that turns slowly toward you, no face yet. *Vance, "Turjan of Miir".* **M**
- **AG. The Asterisms.** ⟳ Stand still looking up for a minute and a few stars brighten, joined by faint lines into four sigils, appearing in the order Pride, Sloth, Avarice, Spite, fading when you move. *Dunsany, The Gods of Pegāna.* **S** (sigil shapes are Austin's to design)
- **AH. The Midden of Plate.** In a tumbled tower lies a heap of coin, chalices and crowns. ⟳ The closer you get the louder it chinks; up close it is dross and verdigris, and the crown on top is lead. *Dunsany, "The Hoard of the Gibbelins"; Avarice.* **M**

## The brainstorm's top five

1. **E. The Vigilant**: patience and great age in one stepped motion, cheap, pays off every glance back.
2. **T. The Speculum**: a young sun seen only in the water is the Dying Earth's elegy in one image.
3. **R. The Parhelion City**: turns the existing guttering into a reward for watching the sun.
4. **AB. The Menology**: puts the game's three-year run into the landscape as something you can touch.
5. **Y. The Settle**: the purest stand-still-and-wait moment, uncanny without any monster.

Needs design input before building: AG's sigils, AC's element pairs, any carved signs on AB. Those are questions for `proposals/`, not things to invent in a build.
