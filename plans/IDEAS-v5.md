# Diversity, life and detail: ideas for wilderness v5

Brainstormed 2026-11-08 for the v5 pass (`wilderness-v5.md`), after Austin's note that the same stones and plants occur everywhere and that the forest, the fens and the blue crystal area are the only places with a personality of their own. v5 is the land between the landmarks: named bioregions, people hiding out in them, small life with a trick, and set-dressing with a story. Letters continue from `IDEAS-v4.md`. Names are **[Proposed]** placeholders. Coordinates are metres (x east, z south); the walkable floor ends at r 300, and the v4 picks (the Great Span at z ≈ 215, the Plinth Road from (130, −150) north, the Hawser down into the Sunken Court, the Foundered Nave at (255, 40)) are taken as built. The sun is fixed low in the west-south-west. Costs: **cheap** under a day, **medium** a few days, **big** a week or more; "terrain" means work in `terrain.js` (biome weights, ground colour, a marked block of heightfield). Austin picks; nothing here is built until he does.

## Bioregions

- **BO. The Bletting** [Proposed]. The orchard country round Medlar Close, spread out past its wall: old medlars and quinces gone to thicket, windfalls browning in the grass, a hedge-line of blackthorn running between broken field walls of honey-coloured stone laid without mortar. The ground is long rank grass with knapweed and the dull purple of self-heal; the air is sweet and slightly rotten, full of drowsy wasps, and the sound is fruit dropping. The boundary is the blackthorn hedge itself, gapped where paths come through. Only here: the pomace snails, fist-sized, their shells a worn gilt lacquer, grazing the windfalls and leaving trails that catch the red light like thread. *Vance's decadent gardens in Kaiin; Hubert Robert's overgrown parks.* Where: a ring about (130, −90) out to r 110, taking in the Pomegranate House and the Leaning Yard, bounded on the north by the Plinth Road. **Medium**, terrain (an orchard weight that already exists, widened, and a hedge mask).
- **BP. The Saltings** [Proposed]. What the Wrong Colour has been doing to the land for a long time: a salt-flat between the fen's east rim and the east wall, a white crust cracked into plates with pink gypsum roses at the joints, glasswort gone blood-red along the runnels, a margin of the same grey brittle reeds that ring the eddy. No stone at all, only crust and a few bleached stumps. Heat shimmer lies on it; the crust creaks underfoot and the wind lifts a fine salt that stings the light. The boundary is a tideline of grey reeds and a step of a metre down from the moor. Only here: the halite crabs, pale and sideways, their backs crusted the colour of the flat, that drop into holes in the plates when you come near and leave star-patterns of pellets round each hole. *Clark Ashton Smith's dead seas in Zothique; Brackett's Martian sea-bottoms.* Where: from about (165, 140) to (260, 150), centred (215, 150), under the east end of the Great Span. **Medium**, terrain (a flat, a crust shader, a step).
- **BQ. The Outwash** [Proposed]. The glacier's foreland: a grey plain of gravel and till in braided dry channels, the stones sorted and rounded, with erratics the size of carts perched on three points where the ice put them down. Cushions of moss-campion with a few pinpricks of pink, map-lichen in yellow-green continents on every boulder, and nothing taller than an ankle. A cold breath comes down off the ice and lays the grass flat; under the stones meltwater ticks. The boundary on the south is a long low ridge of terminal moraine you step up onto and see the gravel beyond. Only here: a white grouse that sits among the erratics so still it reads as a stone, and flushes at three metres with a sound like a pane breaking. *Smith, "The Coming of the White Worm"; Caspar David Friedrich's Sea of Ice.* Where: an apron round the Lattice's south and west, from about (−30, −200) to (100, −195); the Hawser comes down across its west edge. **Medium**, terrain (braided channels in a marked block, a gravel ground colour).
- **BR. The High Ling** [Proposed]. The heath under the eastern wall, the one place that faces the sun across the whole bowl and is lit full on: deep ling and bell-heather, ruddy and knee-high, bilberry in the hollows, and gorse in bloom whose yellow turns ember in this light. Its stone is a pinkish granite that weathers into tors, stacked pillows of rock 6 to 10 m high, with a skirt of clitter fallen round each. Heather pollen hangs in the air; grasshoppers stridulate and the wind moans in the tors' joints. It sits on a shelf: the boundary is a scarp three metres high along its western edge, so you climb into it. Only here: stonechats, whose call is two pebbles knapped together, each on the top sprig of a gorse bush. *Hardy's Egdon Heath in The Return of the Native; Dunsany's moors in The King of Elfland's Daughter.* Where: from about (240, 10) north to (265, −100), clear of the Foundered Nave. **Medium**, terrain (the shelf and its scarp; tors from `rocks.js`).
- **BS. The Clough** [Proposed]. The river's canyon and the spur it cuts, made a world of its own: dripping walls of dark layered slate, hart's-tongue and maidenhair in every seam, liverwort black-green on the wet ledges, a fine mist that the fall sends down the gorge. It is the coolest and dampest place in the bowl and sounds of nothing but water. The boundary is the rim: step over it and the dry moor's sound cuts off. Only here: the dipper, a dark round bird with a white bib that walks straight into the current and along the bed under the water, and comes out dry a few metres on. *Ruskin's drawings of wet rock and fern; Smith's gorges in Averoigne.* Where: the canyon from the Speculum (208, 258) down to the mouth (134, 211) and its rims. **Cheap**, no terrain (planting and wet-rock material on what exists).
- **BT. The Demesne** [Proposed]. The keep's home ground, once a park: turf grazed short and gone tussocky, the outlines of parterres showing as ridges under the grass, yew shapes clipped long ago into cones and urns and now grown shapeless, and dressed blocks scattered from the fallen curtain tower. The boundary is a ha-ha, a sunk wall in a ditch that you do not see until you reach its edge, then a two-metre drop. The air is still and smells of cut stone; rooks talk at the keep. Only here: the peafowl, white and moulted, trailing a few last eyed feathers, crying from the fallen blocks at every gutter. *Peake, Gormenghast's grounds; Vance's courtly ruins in "The Eyes of the Overworld".* Where: a ring round Carcanet Keep from r 42 to about r 100, north and east of the keep (the fen holds the south, the Sward the west). **Medium**, terrain (the ha-ha's ditch in a marked block, parterre ridges in the ground mask).
- **BU. The Lynchets** [Proposed]. The Byre's farmland gone back to grass: long terraces stepped down the slope toward the tarn, ridge-and-furrow under the turf that shows in low light, docks and nettles thick where middens were, and drystone walls of grey field-stone running in straight lines to nowhere, gapped and slumped. Thistle-down drifts in the air; the sound is wind in the dock-heads and a gate creaking somewhere with no gate in sight. The boundary is a wall: every edge of the Lynchets is a wall, with the moor plainly different on the other side. Only here: stoats in half-moult, ermine-white in patches and brown in others, that hunt the furrows and stand up to look at you. *Hardy's Wessex; Paul Nash's ploughed downland.* Where: centred about (−115, 15), r 45, between the Cardinal Stones, the Byre and the Sward; the causeway crosses it. **Medium**, terrain (terrace steps and furrow ridges in a marked block).
- **BV. The Strandline** [Proposed]. The south-east shore of Lees Tarn: a beach of black flint shingle sorted by size into bands, the coarse up top and the fine at the water, with horsetails and bogbean in the shallows and quillwort under the surface. Midges stand in columns over the margin; water laps though there is no wind. The boundary is the change from grass to shingle, abrupt as a carpet's edge. Only here: small grey sandpipers that run in and out in exact step with a tide the tarn has, with no moon to pull it, a hand's breadth up and down every few minutes. *Smith's black-strand shores; Böcklin's Isle of the Dead for the colour of the water.* Where: from about (−30, 120) round to (−70, 170), the tarn's shore not taken by the Sward, the Byre or the wood. **Cheap**, terrain (a shingle ground colour and a beach profile).
- **BW. The Adust** [Proposed]. The apron under the west wall, nearest the sun and burnt by it for ages: clay cracked into hexagons, small drifts of red sand, sandstone pavements varnished black on the sun side, and plants that are only rosettes and cushions, everything leaning a little east as if from heat. The air is dust lit red; stones tick as they expand. The boundary is colour: the moor's green fails over a few metres into ochre and rust, with a last line of dead heather. Only here: beetles that stand on their heads facing the sun, rows of them on each pavement, and step down all at once when it gutters. *Vance's Dying Earth deserts; the Namib fog-basking beetle; Hodgson's "The Night Land", where the near-sun land is scorched.* Where: from about (−275, −60) to (−270, 50), between the Armiger's Tower and the Deodand Wood. **Medium**, terrain (a dry ground colour, crackle in the shader, small dunes).
- **BX. The Clints** [Proposed]. Limestone pavement in the north-west: grey slabs (clints) split by deep fissures (grikes), level as a floor and bare, with ferns and bloody cranesbill growing in the grikes where the wind cannot reach and nowhere else, so from above it reads as grey with green lines. Water drips in the grikes; the air is still and dry and smells of wet stone. The boundary is a pale step where the slabs break the turf. Only here: the olm, a pale blind salamander as long as a forearm, seen only by looking down into a grike when the sun gutters and its skin catches what light there is. *The Burren; Hodgson's "The House on the Borderland" for the pit-country; Redon's pale things in the dark.* Where: centred about (−175, −215), r 40, north of the Wayside Shrine and east of the Armiger's Tower. **Medium**, terrain (a pavement block, grike lines in the shader).

## Humanoids

- **BY. The Taker of Altitudes** [Proposed]. An old man in a faded orange robe on a red pavement of the Adust, sighting the sun along a staff set with a brass quadrant, then writing a figure in a book on his knee. The sun does not move, so he writes the same figure each time, and the book is thick with it. He ignores you; stand between him and the sun and he lowers the staff and waits, without impatience, until you move. Sound: the quadrant's click, a murmured number. *Vance's Turjan and his dying-age savants; Dürer's Melencolia I.* Where: the Adust, about (−255, 10), facing the sun. **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **BZ. The Coheirs** [Proposed]. Two women, identical, in identical dresses of a once-fine purple gone brown, sitting on the shingle with a net stretched on a frame between them. One mends; the other, facing her, unpicks the same mesh. When you come within 20 m they stop together, turn their heads to you together, and go back to work together. Sound: a hum in thirds, and the net's knots pulled tight. *Peake, Titus Groan: Cora and Clarice, the twin aunts; Penelope's web.* Where: the Strandline, about (−45, 160). **Medium**, no terrain.
- **CA. The Exciseman** [Proposed]. A thin man in a high collar at a folding desk where a path comes through the Bletting's blackthorn, a ledger open before him and a strongbox at his feet with no bottom. As you pass he stands, bows, writes a line, and names a tariff in a coin nobody has struck for an age; he does not stop you. Pass without stopping and he writes for longer. Sound: the nib's scratch, a dry cough, the box's empty clang. *Vance's toll-takers and petty officials in "The Eyes of the Overworld"; Peake's Barquentine and the Book of Ritual.* Where: the Bletting's south-west gap, about (70, −75), on the lead from the keep to Medlar Close. **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CB. The Long Sleeper** [Proposed]. Under a single old medlar outside the close, a man asleep on his back in robes of a cut nobody wears, his beard grown into the grass and a ring of brown toadstools round him. He breathes once a minute, deeply enough to see. He never wakes; come close and his eyelids move as if he dreamed you. Sound: the breath, and a fruit dropping onto him now and then. *Dunsany, "The Sword of Welleran", where the old heroes sleep; Burne-Jones's sleepers in The Briar Rose.* Where: the Bletting, about (180, −40). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CC. The Penumbral Man** [Proposed]. A tall figure that exists only while the sun gutters: when the light dips, he is standing where the largest tor's shadow ends, quite still, and when it comes back he is gone. Each gutter the shadow and he are in the same place. From far off he is a man in a long coat; from near, in the dim, his outline is a little too long at the neck. Sound: none; the stonechats go quiet. *Hodgson's Silent Ones; Redon's charcoal noirs.* Where: the High Ling, about (262, −65), seen best from the Leaning Yard. **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CD. The Little Thurifer** [Proposed]. A child-sized figure in robes three times too long, the hem dragging metres behind, swinging a censer that gives off a thin blue smoke, walking a sheep-track between the Wayside Shrine and the Cardinal Stones and back. From far off a child; up close, under the hood, the face is old and unfinished, like something taken from a vat early. Approach and it steps off the track, bows far too low, and waits for you to pass. Sound: the censer's chain, the robes' long hiss on the grass. *Vance's homunculi in "Turjan of Miir"; Goya's Caprichos, the dwarf clerics.* Where: from the shrine (−108, −128) to the stones (−170, −50). **Medium**, no terrain.
- **CE. The Bather** [Proposed]. Seen only from the canyon's rim: a woman eight metres tall reclining in the slow pool below the second rapid, pale shoulders and hair in the water, washing one arm with slow care. She never looks up. Climb down into the Clough and there are only long pale stones in the river and the water moving round them. Sound: from the rim, water poured from a cupped hand; from below, the river. *Baudelaire, "La Géante"; Goya, The Colossus.* Where: the pool at about (158, 228), seen from the right-bank rim near (165, 215). **Medium**, no terrain (a swap between two models by the player's height).
- **CF. The Sexton of No One** [Proposed]. A man in black with a spade, digging graves in neat rows on the south moor: forty or so open, square, each the same depth, none with a stone, none filled. He digs one more each gutter. Approach and he stops, leans on the spade, measures you from head to foot with a look, and goes back to digging. Sound: the spade's bite, earth thrown. *Smith, "The Empire of the Necromancers"; Hamlet's clown without his jokes.* Where: south of the Great Span, about (55, 272). **Medium**, terrain (the grave cuts are shallow decals, or a marked block).
- **CG. The Leerie** [Proposed]. A lamplighter with a long brass pole and a small flame at its tip, walking a line of iron lamp-standards along an old kerb. At each he reaches up, touches the flame to the wick, waits, and walks on; none of them lights. Only when the sun gutters do the whole row light at once behind him, for those few seconds. He never looks back to see it. Sound: footsteps, the pole's click on the lamp-cage. *R. L. Stevenson's "The Lamplighter"; Peake, rituals kept for nobody; Hodgson's Last Redoubt.* Where: a line of twelve standards from the Pomegranate House toward the Arcature, about (110, −25) to (145, 35). **Medium**, no terrain (the lamps fit v4's light kit).
- **CH. The Husbandman** [Proposed]. From the far side of the Lynchets, a bent figure hoeing a terrace, slow and steady, a wide hat, the hoe rising and falling. At 30 m it is clear the hoe is its arm, jointed twice, and the hat is part of the head. It goes on hoeing a furrow that has had nothing in it for an age. Come closer than 15 m and it stops and stands, which is worse, and resumes when you leave. Sound: the hoe's chock in dry earth. *Hodgson's things that are nearly men; Goya's Caprichos ("Hasta su abuelo").* Where: the Lynchets, about (−135, 30). **Medium**, no terrain.
- **CI. The Stylite** [Proposed]. On top of the tallest tor of the High Ling sits a gaunt man in a loincloth, cross-legged, facing the sun, unmoving. A rope runs from his hand down the tor's face to a basket at the foot. When you come near, the basket rises, empty, to his hand and then lowers again, as if offered. He never looks down. Sound: the rope's creak. *Flaubert's The Temptation of Saint Anthony in Redon's lithographs; Dunsany's hermits.* Where: the High Ling's highest tor, about (268, −80). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CJ. The Masquer** [Proposed]. In a green theatre of turf banks on the Demesne, a figure in a loose white costume, ruff and black skullcap, dancing a slow measure alone, bowing to partners who are not there. It dances only while unwatched: look at it and it holds the pose, mid-step, for as long as you look. Up close the costume is grey with age and the face is a mask with nothing behind the eyeholes. Sound: a few bars of a viol from nowhere, which stop when it stops. *Beardsley's Pierrots; Poe's "The Masque of the Red Death"; Watteau's Gilles.* Where: the Demesne, north of the keep, about (20, −55). **Medium**, terrain (a ring of turf banks).
- **CK. The Angler on the Salt** [Proposed]. A man in oilskins and a wide-brimmed hat on a stool in the middle of the Saltings, casting a long line out over the dry crust and reeling it slowly in. Every so often the rod bends hard and he plays something for a while; then the line comes in empty and he casts again. He ignores you entirely. Sound: the reel, the line's whistle, a grunt when it bends. *Smith's dead-sea ports; Dunsany, "Idle Days on the Yann".* Where: the Saltings, about (230, 145). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CL. The Accrescent** [Proposed]. A man walking south out of the Lattice at one step an hour, his trailing half grown through with glacial-blue crystal from heel to shoulder, his front half still flesh and robes. Each step leaves a glassy footprint that stays. Come close and he does not see you; his eyes are on the keep. Sound: a faint tinkle like ice in a glass at each step. *Smith, "The Coming of the White Worm"; Merritt, The Metal Monster.* Where: the Outwash, about (60, −180), facing south. **Cheap**, no terrain (one figure, a footprint trail).

## Little characters

- **CM. The Indistinct** [Proposed]. Something small and dark that keeps exactly seven metres from you along the ha-ha's ditch, always a little out of focus whatever you do: a soft blur where the eye wants an animal. Walk toward it and it keeps the distance; stop and it stops. *Walter de la Mare, "The Listeners"; Redon's floating forms.* Where: the Demesne's ha-ha, north and east of the keep. **Cheap**, no terrain (a blur shader on one sprite).
- **CN. The Testudines** [Proposed]. Among the Clints' loose blocks, some of the rounder grey stones are tortoises, shells weathered and lichened to match, motionless. When the sun gutters three or four of them get up and walk a few metres and settle as stones again. *Smith's Zothique, where everything is older than it looks; Dunsany's slow gods.* Where: the Clints, about (−180, −205). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CO. The Repetend** [Proposed]. A brown bird on the Lynchets' walls that follows you along the wall-tops and, a second behind, repeats your footsteps exactly, gravel or grass, walking or hurrying. Stop and it plays out your last few steps, then is quiet. *The lyrebird; de la Mare's echoing houses.* Where: the Lynchets' walls, about (−110, −5). **Cheap**, no terrain (an audio delay on the footstep cue).
- **CP. The Scriveners** [Proposed]. A shoal of small dark fish in the tarn's north shallows that arranges itself, for a breath at a time, into one of the Hypogeum's glyphs, holds it, and breaks up. The glyph is the one the bronze door showed last. *Smith, "The Last Hieroglyph"; Dunsany's lettered waters.* Where: Lees Tarn's north shallows, about (−100, 58). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CQ. The Simulacrum** [Proposed]. Winter gnats dancing in columns over the Outwash's meltwater pools. When the sun gutters, one column draws together into a face, a long grave face the size of a man's, that looks at you and comes apart when the light comes back. *Hodgson's faces in the dark; Arcimboldo's composed heads.* Where: the Outwash's pools, about (20, −205). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CR. The Pearl Road** [Proposed]. A file of red ants crosses the causeway along the joints of the hex paving, and in the middle of the file six of them carry a single pearl the size of a pea, steadily, toward somewhere off in the grass. Step on the road and the file parts round your foot and closes again. *Vance's small jewelled things; Smith's "The Weird of Avoosl Wuthoqquan", where wealth goes where it goes.* Where: the causeway in the Lynchets, about (−140, −30). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CS. The Hares in Conclave** [Proposed]. Brown hares grazing the open grass between Medlar Close and the Leaning Yard. When the sun gutters they stop, sit up, and face inward in a ring, eleven of them, perfectly still; when the light returns they scatter. *Vance's beasts that know something; the hare as a witch's shape in English folk belief.* Where: the Bletting, about (185, −75). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CT. The Tesserine Moles** [Proposed]. Molehills on the Demesne's old lawn, but the earth they push up is full of tesserae: small dressed cubes of red, gold and lapis from a mosaic floor somewhere under the turf. Now and then a new heap rises, slowly, with a click of stone on stone. *Vance's buried splendours; the mosaics of Ravenna for the colours.* Where: the Demesne, about (75, 0). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CU. The Trigon Spinners** [Proposed]. Spiders in the Great Span's pier feet and the lowest arches spin webs that are not orbs but the Span's own recursive triangles, each strand bright in the red light. Brush one and the spider begins the same pattern again, from the outside in. *Arachne by way of Escher; the Span's own etching.* Where: the feet of the Great Span's piers, along z ≈ 215. **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CV. The Brand-Kites** [Proposed]. Rust-coloured kites that wheel over the Adust and drop low to pick up smouldering twigs from the hottest pavements, then fly them off to set a patch of dry cushion-plants alight and hunt what runs out. Thin smoke rises here and there across the region. *The Australian firehawks; Vance's sun that burns the land at its edge.* Where: the Adust, about (−260, −20). **Medium**, no terrain (ember particles, small smoke columns).
- **CW. The Ephemerids** [Proposed]. Mayflies that hatch from the tarn's south-east shallows each time the sun gutters, a cloud of pale wings rising into the dim, and are dead on the water when it comes back: the surface flecked white for a minute, then clear. *Vance's world-span of a day; Smith's short-lived creatures under the late sun.* Where: the Strandline's shallows, about (−35, 135). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CX. The Toadstone** [Proposed]. A fat toad on a wet ledge of the Clough with a red stone set in its forehead like a cabochon, which glows faintly when the sun gutters. It never moves. If you look away and back it has turned to face you. *The toadstone of English lore; Smith's jewelled creatures.* Where: the Clough's west wall, about (180, 230). **Cheap**, no terrain.

## Detail

- **CY. The Collation** [Proposed]. In a clearing inside Medlar Close a long table is laid for twelve: pewter plates with a bletted medlar on each, glasses with dust in them, the chairs pulled out as if the guests had just stood. One chair is knocked over. *Peake's feast in Titus Groan; Dickens's Miss Havisham.* Where: Medlar Close, about (150, −70). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **CZ. The Post Cairn** [Proposed]. A cairn at the top of the High Ling where a path heads for the Gradine. Under its top stone, folded and sealed, a letter addressed in a fine hand; the ink has run and the name cannot be read. *The cairns of polar parties, where notes were left for whoever came; Dunsany's unread messages.* Where: the High Ling's north end, about (255, −140). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **DA. The Drying Green** [Proposed]. A line strung between two erratics on the Outwash, hung with robes of thin silk in saffron and violet, stiff with frost, swinging in the cold air from the ice. They are fresh; someone pegged them out. *Vance's Kaiin silks; Friedrich's frozen rigging.* Where: the Outwash, about (35, −195). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **DB. The Dry Skiff** [Proposed]. A small boat drawn up on the red sand of the Adust, a long way from any water: oars shipped, an eye painted on the prow, and in its bilge a few centimetres of water that reflects the sun. *Smith's ships on dead sea-beds; Tanguy's dry shores.* Where: the Adust, about (−250, 55). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **DC. The Discontinued Track** [Proposed]. A line of bare footprints crossing the Saltings' crust from the reed margin toward the middle, firm and evenly spaced, that stops. No turn, no stumble, nothing beyond. *Hodgson's footprints that end; the Devonshire "Devil's Footprints".* Where: the Saltings, from (170, 150) east to about (215, 160). **Cheap**, no terrain (decals).
- **DD. The Tenanted Window** [Proposed]. A low house half buried in the Clints, only its roof and one window above the slabs. The window is lit, warm, every time; nobody is ever seen behind it, but the curtain is drawn a little differently on each visit. *Hodgson's Last Redoubt windows; Hopper's lit rooms.* Where: the Clints' north edge, about (−160, −235). **Cheap**, no terrain (one emissive window in v4's light budget).
- **DE. The Slipper** [Proposed]. A single brocade slipper on the causeway, the toe curled, the gold thread rubbed through, lying on its side as if dropped from something moving. It is on the first walk out. *Vance's courtly gallants; Cinderella only by accident.* Where: the causeway, about (−20, 26). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **DF. The Unfinished Prospect** [Proposed]. An easel at the brow of the High Ling looking west over the whole bowl, with a canvas of that exact view, carefully done, except that the painted sun is small and white and the sky blue. Stool, paints dried in their dishes, a brush across the palette. *The Speculum's young sun; Hubert Robert's views of the Louvre in ruins.* Where: the High Ling's brow, about (238, −30). **Cheap**, no terrain.
- **DG. The Long Figure** [Proposed]. On the lowest slope of the east wall, facing the sun, a figure 80 m long cut through the turf to pale stone: a standing person with too many arms, or a person with their arms in several places at once, as in a slow photograph. Seen best from the Span's deck and from the keep. *The Long Man of Wilmington and the Cerne giant; Marey's chronophotographs.* Where: about (292, 70), above the Foundered Nave. **Cheap**, terrain (a mask in the ground shader).
- **DH. The Surveyor's Pegs** [Proposed]. A line of wooden pegs, each with a faded ribbon, runs dead straight from the north wall to the south wall, ignoring everything: through the Lynchets' walls, across the causeway, into Lees Tarn and out the other side, under the Great Span. Every hundred metres, a peg with a brass cap. The surveyor is not to be found. *Vance's projects abandoned by their sponsors; Borges's map of the empire.* Where: along x = −80 from (−80, −285) to (−80, 285). **Cheap**, no terrain.

## Light and illumination

- **DI. The Tinctures** [Proposed]. Each bioregion takes the one sun differently, so the haze and the ground answer it in their own colour: rose-white glare off the Saltings, amber wasp-haze in the Bletting, gold pollen over the High Ling, red dust in the Adust, a blue-white breath off the Outwash, a green-grey mist in the Clough, flat pewter on the Clints, silver thistle-down in the Lynchets. You can tell where you are by the colour of the air ahead before you can see the ground. This builds on v4's fog tiers and its brighter floor: same luminance, different hue. *Turner's coloured airs; Vance's skies "of dark blue and maroon".* Where: every bioregion, blended across the boundaries over 20–30 m. **Cheap**, no terrain (fog and ambient tint by region in `atmosphere.js`).
- **DJ. The Bologna Stone** [Proposed]. ## Rules of the pass

1. **Every creature and person belongs to one place.** The bioregion creatures live only in their region, the humanoids keep to their spot, and meeting any of them tells you where you are (the v2 rule, extended to the land between the landmarks).
2. **Nothing cute.** Small life is wary, indifferent or slightly wrong; the people are absorbed in a task nobody needs any more. Nothing waves, nothing begs, nothing is a mascot.
3. **Nothing medieval.** Robes, ledgers, quadrants, silk, brass and stone, in the late-age register of the v4 tone note; no tabards, no heraldry, no timber halls, no peasants in hoods.
4. **Everything old and once rich.** Gilt worn to brass, purple to brown, a mosaic in a molehill. Each region shows what it was before what it is; each person keeps a duty from an age that has gone.
5. **Budget by region.** One ground-cover and stone set per bioregion, swapped in rather than added to; one instanced mesh per creature kind; most people a single animated figure with one cue. A region that needs a new draw call gives one back by thinning the generic moor it replaces. Light is emissive or baked glow first; a real point light only where a thing must light its surroundings or throw a moving shadow (the Link-Boy, the Kept Fire).
