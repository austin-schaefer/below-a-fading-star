## The story, as built

The Collation is laid for twelve and one chair is gone. Someone dragged it out of the clearing, carried it across the map, setting it down to rest three times, and stood it at the head of the grave the Sexton is digging, facing the grave, with its place's pewter plate and glass on the seat and a black coat over its back.

## Why it is hard to read

At the table a visitor sees a gap, two rounds on the cloth and two furrows: nobody is in that picture yet, and the trail is four dents in the turf. The ending is good, and few arrive at it knowing what it ends. That is what a deep story costs.

## The story behind it (written by the orchestrator when the library began; the owner's to change)

Twelve have dined together at this table for longer than anyone remembers, and the rule of the table is that it is always laid for twelve. One of them has died. The eleven could not bear the empty chair and could not break the rule, so they carried his place to him: his chair, his plate, his glass, his coat, set at the head of his grave so that he still sits down with them. They pour for him. They are not good at grief and they are fond of rules.
