# The Reckoner under the door

- **Kind:** plain
- **State:** built: beats 1 to 4. Beat 5 is a seed
- **Someone:** the Reckoner, who keeps the count of the sun's failures behind the Hypogeum's bronze door and has not come up in a very long time. Never seen
- **Places:** the Hypogeum (its stair, door and lintel); the mother crystal at the heart of the Lattice; the thread of light between them
- **Touches:** [The gold from the court](the-gold-from-the-court.md) (gold tesserae); the Tesserine Moles' tessera with a glyph on the Demesne's lawn and the Scriveners in Lees Tarn (`src/v7/glyphs/`), which both carry the door's glyphs

## The story

When the sun first faltered, a polity long gone appointed a Reckoner to keep its account: to note every gutter, against the day it would not come back. They cut the Hypogeum for him, a stair down to a bronze door, and he went in with his lamps and his tables and had the door closed behind him. The stair goes on far below the door. On the lintel outside he keeps his count where anyone may read it: eleven glyphs, and at every gutter he turns the row one place from within. He has been at it so long that his count runs in gold, one tessera a thousand gutters, cut from a mosaic floor somewhere down there.

He is not dead and not asleep, only far down and busy. Knock, and he answers from the depths; knock again another time and he has climbed to hear you; the third time he is waiting just behind the bronze, and he runs the whole row through for you, so you can see the count is kept. Every time, he pushes a tessera out under the door: a receipt, or a fee, or a tally of his own of who has come. The door does not open. It is not that kind of door.

The mother crystal drinks from the sun. Lay a hand on it and it pulls a gutter out of the sun before its time, and the Reckoner, far off, dutifully turns his row for a gutter that was not his to count. Then the crystal sends a thread of its light out over the moor, down his stair and under his door, and his glyphs glow its blue from then on. Whether he is pleased is not known.

## What it ties together

The gutter (the one shared event) with the lintel (which counts it), the crystal (which can force one) and the gold tesserae that turn up in the jackdaw's nest and the moles' heaps: they could all have come up from under the door. A next agent could let a tessera be carried off by the jackdaw, or have the moles' heap move nearer the Hypogeum.

## What the visitor never learns

His name, how far down the stair goes, what he eats, and what happens at the gutter that does not recover.

## Open threads

- The astronomer walks the world without a story: he may be the Reckoner's correspondent, or his successor in waiting.
- The tesserae are gold from a mosaic floor: the Sunken Court's sun lost its gold to the jackdaw. Two floors, or one?
- The lens mount's door, elsewhere, stands ajar and lit: someone did come out of somewhere.
