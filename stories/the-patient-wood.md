## The story

The north wood is younger than the old one, and some of what stands in it is not a tree. Five of them came to the grove long ago, tall as the trunks round them, on legs like boles with roots at the feet and boughs held up with leaves on them, and they have been standing there since, watching whatever comes up the track. They are not hungry and they are not angry. They are curious, and very old, and they will not be seen moving: a visitor who looks away and back finds a trunk where there was none, or a knot that was looking at the ground now looking at them. When the sun gutters they all come at once, and when it catches they are standing round the visitor in a ring, faces in, still, with the way out left open behind. One of the five has been still so long it has taken root; something nested in its fork, and it lets it.

Lower down, in the old wood and along the tracks, smaller things keep watch the same way: a pair of pale eyes between the trunks that shuts when you look at it, and opens again somewhere else. At the coloured glass to the west the moths come in a cloud to the light inside, big as a hand, an eye on each wing, and beat on the panes all night.

## What it ties together

The wood's two watchers that move only unseen (the deodand at the edge, the stilters inside) are one habit of the place, not one creature: whatever lives under this sun has learned not to be caught moving. A grafter's wood (the Graffer) is a wood in which a tree may be something else grafted on.
