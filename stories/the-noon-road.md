## The story

The Heliarchs built the Nave to face the sun, and set it in gold on the east wall for the days it did not show. They looked at it until it began to fail.

The people after them stopped looking. The Noonwrights meant to keep it: they hung their collectors in the sky above the haze, gathered the daylight while there was daylight, and sent it down in tubes to be stored and shared out, a measure at a time, in great sealed carriers of milk glass, each marked with a band of ox-blood and a row of empty rings. One of their lines came down out of the sky through the Nave's roof, turned in a great elbow slung in spun filament from the capitals, and ran east down the Heliarchs' nave, a hand clear of the colonnade, a length to a bay; a lesser line left it by the south door. They cut no stone to do it. They did not care about the sun on the wall, and somebody after them cared enough to pick out its gold.

Then the line stopped. Nobody knows what stopped it. The roof came down where the elbow went up through it; the black water rose through the floor; the tube broke bay by bay and its carriers floated up inside it, still coupled nose to tail, and have ridden there since, between the hoops that are all that stands of the line. Most are dark. A few still hold their measure, and the light in them is the light of a noon nobody alive has seen: white-gold, steady, lying in the belly of the glass like something poured.

Somebody still comes for it. The shears on the rim, the hung stair, the stage lashed to the junction's back, the planks over every coupling are theirs, and so are the flasks on the stage and the bulb in its rope cradle by the shears, a third full, waiting to be carried off. The Leerie lights the valley's lamps and the tender oils the lattices with the same lamp oil, and neither has ever said where it is drawn.

## What it ties together

The Nave (a hole with a hall in it and no reason), the lattices (built by nobody now remembered: by these), the tender and the Leerie (their oil), and the tesserae (the sun on the wall, robbed). It gives the valley a fourth people between the Heliarchy and the folk here now, and the walk its science: a people who tried to bank the sun. A next agent can dress a lattice in rings and collector cells, bring the Leerie down the stair at dusk with a flask, let a visitor tap a carrier and carry a light up (an act), or follow the lesser line south.

## What the visitor never learns

What stopped the line. How much noon is left. Who comes for it.
