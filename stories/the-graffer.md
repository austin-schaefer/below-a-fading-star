## The story

Six people of a rich house were laid in a vault under the wood: a lady with her fan, an armiger with his sword, a child with a hoop, a fat magnate with his cup, two lovers in one tomb, a falconer. Their gardener would not have it. He built a house of coloured glass over the vault, hung a flask of stored noon in it because the sun no longer grows anything, and let the roots of what he planted go down through the floor into the tombs. What came up is the six of them again, as flowers: the fan is a leaf, the cup a bloom, the hawk a bud on a wrist. They turn their heads to whoever walks round the glass, as they once turned to the sun. He locked the door from inside and has not been seen. Below, a seventh tomb stands open and empty with its lid half carved, a watering can on it; above, in a seventh pot, something small has come up.

## What it ties together

The owner's two ideas for the wood (a glasshouse of flower-people nobody can enter; tunnels and crypts) are one place seen from above and from below. The wood gets a keeper, as the graves have the Sexton and the Garth has whoever waters it.

## What the visitor never learns

Whether the Graffer is in the seventh tomb's pot by his own doing, or is simply late.

## Open threads

- The rich house: whose were the six? The keep's, the Pomegranate House's?
- The can is the Garth's make. Is the Graffer the one who waters there?
- Where the flask came from: the Nave's carriers are short one.
