# Noon by the jug

- **Kind:** plain
- **State:** built

## The story

The Lappet hangs on the north-east of the keep's hill and the sun is low in the west, so no house in it has ever had the sun on its wall. What it has is the fountain: a Noonwright vessel's broken foot in the square that wells a little stored daylight instead of water. An old woman draws it. She kneels at nobody's leave on the kerb, bends over the rim with a clay jug, holds it under until it is full, lifts it and looks into it to see that it has taken, and sets it by her knee with the others; then she carries them up the steps on a yoke to whoever has paid, a jug a house, and on the Rungs they stand it in the window. On the Low Rung a child sits on the edge of her roof with her legs over the lane and a basket let down on a cord with an empty jug in it, jiggling it, waiting for the noon-drawer to come by; anyone who walks under the basket is looked at hard, in case they are her.

## What it ties together

Why a hillside village in the keep's shadow shows lit windows by day; what the fountain is for; where the Leerie's oil and the tender's come from (the same noon, drawn somewhere else by someone else). The child and the drawer are two ends of one errand a visitor can join up by walking from one to the other (about 30 m and two stairs).

## What the visitor never learns

What it costs a house, and whether the drawer is also the one who chalks the Leerie's slate on the Swape's post.
