# The tally at the Drum

- **Kind:** plain
- **State:** built
- **Someone:** whoever keeps the waystation (never seen), and every traveller who ever stopped there, the visitor included
- **Places:** the Drum, beside the causeway halfway from Carcanet Keep to the Cardinal Stones, about (-61, 5) (`src/v9/waystation.js`)
- **Touches:** the slipper's mate (he passed this way, or meant to); the Twk-men (who would know who keeps it)

## The story

A Heliarch column fell full length beside the road long ago. The Late folk hollowed one of its drums into a house: plaster on its open end, a horseshoe door, an amber lamp, an awning, a bench, a brazier with a kettle always on. Whoever keeps it counts the travellers who stop, a charcoal stroke each on the plaster by the door, five to a gate. The top rows are full. Row by row there are fewer, until the last rows are single strokes further and further apart: the road is emptying. The keeper still keeps the kettle hot and still sets one cup out on the bench.

When the visitor comes near and looks away, a new stroke appears at the end of the count, blacker than the rest. They have been counted, and nobody was seen doing it.

## What it ties together

The causeway as a road people once used in numbers: the keep, the stones, the pedlars. The Late folk making homes in the Heliarchy's wreckage (spolia, recorded with `ctx.strata.place`).

## What the visitor never learns

Who keeps it. The kettle is hot and the bed is made, and there is never anyone there.
