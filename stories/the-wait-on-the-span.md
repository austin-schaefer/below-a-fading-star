## The story

He stopped at the first lip and laid out what he would carry in a row, nine strides from the way over. She had crossed an hour before him. She went along the stone that is left on the north side, a stride wide over fifty-six metres of air, over the coping that lies across the crack, along the cornice stones hung on their iron, and she did not look down; every few strides she tied a strip of the cloth to the edge of the way, and a strip on a staff at each lip, so that he could see from the first lip where the way went; and she sat on the far side to wait because she knew he was careful and would be slow. She set up what a person sets up who means to wait a few days: a coping stone fallen against the parapet for a back, a sheet of their saffron cloth (cream-lined, madder-bordered, the same as his cloak) thrown over a rope between two poles and pegged on the stone, a strip of it hung at the end against the wind, her bedroll, a second cup the pair of his, the other volume of his book (they had read it aloud in turns, one volume each), a hearth of seven stones. She tied a long strip of the same cloth to a staff and stood it at the west end of the deck, where the cornice stones come up, so that he would see it from the far lip. She chalked a day on the stone each time the sun went down.

Forty-one strokes. The strip is still on the staff, still flying. The cup is on the blanket, the book is face down where she put it when she heard something, and the fire has been cold a long time. She is not here. Whether she went on east, or back, or walked off the end, the camp does not say.
