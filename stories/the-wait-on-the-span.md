## The story

He stopped at the first lip and laid out what he would carry in a row, nine strides from the way over. She had crossed an hour before him. She went along the stone that is left on the north side, a stride wide over fifty-six metres of air, and she did not look down, and she sat on the far side to wait because she knew he was careful and would be slow. She set up what a person sets up who means to wait a few days: a coping stone fallen against the parapet for a back, a sheet of saffron cloth on two poles, her bedroll, a second cup the pair of his, the other volume of his book (they had read it aloud in turns, one volume each), a hearth of seven stones. She tied a strip of the same cloth to a staff and stood it at the west end of the deck so that he would see it from the lip. She chalked a day on the stone each time the sun went down.

Forty-one strokes. The strip is still on the staff. The cup is on the blanket, the book is face down where she put it when she heard something, and the fire has been cold a long time. She is not here. Whether she went on east, or back, or walked off the end, the camp does not say.
