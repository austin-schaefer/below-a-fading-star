## The story

The Noonwrights' lines came down out of the sky and met at junctions before they went on to the stores, the lamps and the garden. The great junctions were spheres of their glass, blown whole, each with a keeper sat at its centre on spun stays, who turned the wheels that sent the noon one way or another.

When the sun went red and the collectors in the sky gathered less and less, the keeper at the west wall's junction turned his wheels shut, one by one, until all the noon that came down stayed with him. He sits there still, at the centre of the last full noon in the valley, both hands on the last wheel, which he turned to shut. The sphere is full of white daylight. Everything the line used to feed is going dark: the garden under the south slope dies a ring at a time on what was left in the tube.

## What it ties together

The Noonwrights' lines (the Nave, the Wellward Way, the Lappet's main, the garden's tube) get a centre and a cause for their stopping; the western moor gets its find. A next agent can let a visitor into the sphere (it is a story of one man keeping a thing to himself, so getting in should cost something), bring the sky's tube (#56) down into its top, or make the hoops a walkable line to the garden.

## What the visitor never learns

Whether he is dead; whether he shut it for himself or to save the last of it; whether he could open it again.
