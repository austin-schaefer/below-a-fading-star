## The story

The Elder builders cut the sign of the sun into a slab beside their road: a V, and a seat at its bottom for a disc they never set there. Long after, the Late folk, who fear whatever walks the moor at night and cannot leave an empty thing alone, put a painted ogee arch on the slab's shoulders and hung from it, on chains, a lantern as big as a room, exactly where the disc should be. A traveller who reaches it at nightfall takes off his shoes, climbs in, winches himself up, pulls the ladder after him and lights the lamp.

Tonight someone is up there. His clogs stand side by side by the winch with his staff against it, the rope is made fast, and the lamp is lit. From the road you can see his shadow on the cloth, lying with his knees drawn up. When the sun gutters he turns the lamp up.

## What it ties together

The Elder slab, the Late folk's arch and lamp, and the sign of the sun in its notch made into a lamp: one site with two hands on it. The shoes left side by side answer the traveller's boots on the Span: here the shoes mean sleep.

## What the visitor never learns

Who he is. He never comes down while anyone is watching.
