# Until the sun goes out

- **Kind:** plain
- **State:** built (v12-H4)
- **Someone:** the last one kept in the Galleries of Durance, sentenced, as everyone there was, to be kept "until the sun goes out"; never seen. And the warders, gone, whose round the lift still makes
- **Places:** the Galleries of Durance on the west wall: the lift and its lower station west of the Armiger's Tower, the gallery at 135 m, its last cell before the ragged end
- **Touches:** [The Reckoner under the door](the-reckoner.md) (he counts the sun's failures; the one in the cell watched for its end)

## The story

The Galleries of Durance were a prison hung on the cliff, and their judges passed one sentence: to be kept until the sun goes out. When the sun was young that was a way of saying for ever. The warders made their round in the lift, tier by tier, with the ring of keys on its hook in the cage. In time the warders stopped coming up, and the kept went, or died, or were forgotten; their grilles hang open, or lie on the deck. The lift still makes the round by itself: down past the lower tiers, a stop at each, and back up to the gallery, its lamp lit and the keys swinging, nobody in it.

One grille is still locked. The one kept behind it took the sentence at its word: the day the sun went out would be the day of their release, and they wanted to see it come. But the cells face east into the cliff's shade, and the sun is behind the mountain. So they dug toward it, low in the back wall, west and a little up, with a spoon: not a way out, a bore a hand wide, a way to look. They got through. The sun still comes in by it, a thin red rod of light across the cell and out through the bars, to a coin of light on the gallery's iron deck. The spoon lies worn to a stub at the end of the stone bench, the spoil is swept into a heap under the hole, and a blanket is folded at the bench's head, under the hole, where a head lay to watch. Nobody is there. The grille is locked.

## What it ties together

The lift that moves "with no visible animating force" (the Galleries since v5) gets a reason: it is the warders' round, still made. The sun's end, which the Reckoner counts toward in his door, is the release the last of the kept was watching for. A next agent could put a second hole somewhere on the far side of the wall, or a spoon in the jackdaw's nest.

## What the visitor never learns

Who they were, what they did, whether they saw the sun every day through the hole until they died, or whether the grille was locked from inside.

## Open threads

- The keys in the cage: does one of them fit the locked grille? Nobody has tried.
