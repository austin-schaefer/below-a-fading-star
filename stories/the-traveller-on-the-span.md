## The story

He came to cross the Span and found it broken. He was not a man who turned back. He took his boots off and set them side by side, folded his cloak square, opened his pack and laid out a tin cup, a book and a coil of rope in a row, the way a man does who is deciding what to carry. He ran the rope out to the edge. It hangs a forearm's length over the drop: it was never going to be long enough, and he found that out. That was years ago. He is on the slope below, on his back on his greatcoat, barefoot, his stick broken and his purse burst. Nobody has touched the coins. His hat got further than he did.
