# Wilderness runtime reference

The URL params, the quality tiers, the shadow cadence, the profiler and what each test checks. Paths are relative to the repository root. Every agent that adds a flag appends it to its milestone's group here (v6: "The v6 switches"; v7: "The v7 living"); keep-both merges have doubled whole groups before, so grep for duplicates after a merge.

## URL params

### General

- `pos=x,z`
- `yaw=`
- `pitch=` (radians; yaw 0 looks north along -z, π/2 looks west along -x)
- `q=high|medium|low` (or `tier=`; pins the tier for this load)
- `only=vegetation,water` to load only some modules
- `skip=audio`
- `wind=0.5` to scale the wind (the audio and the plants' sway; `wind=0` holds the plants still)
- `perf=1` (profiler)
- `bench=1|<seconds>` (`measure=1`: the settings panel's Measure row started it; the bench takes `bench`, `perf` and `measure` off the URL when done)
- `machine=<name>`
- `bloom=0`
- `shadows=0`
- `gpu=0` (profile as if the GPU timer were missing)
- `debug=1`
- `tinct=0` (no regional tint on the haze and the light)
- `placegrade=0` (no per-place exposure, saturation, contrast and warmth in the finish, v8-02); `shafts=0` (no ember shafts in the wood)
- `gutter=<len>,<every>` (seconds; pins the sun-gutter's length and interval and starts the first one within one interval; `gutter=v3` restores the old 6.5-9 s every 70-150 s; the default is 12-16 s every 60-110 s, timings exported from `src/sky.js`)

### v3 stage 2 switches (each defaults to the new behaviour; see `plans/wilderness-v3.md`)

- `cap=60` (frames per second; `0` uncapped)
- `idle=0` (full rate under the cards and when unfocused)
- `dpr=` (pixel ratio; default high 1.25, low 0.85)
- `shadowhz=2` (`0` redraws the shadow map every frame)
- `shadowdyn=15` (box-move redraws a second while turning, and creature-pose redraws; default 10 on integrated GPUs)
- `shadowcull=0` (far creatures keep their shadows)
- `pcf=soft|hard`
- `bloomres=0.5` (`1` full size)
- `bloomtune=strength,radius,threshold`
- `bloomcap=16`: the ceiling on bloom's bright pass (brightest channel; NaN and Inf drop to black), `src/bloomfix.js`; `0` is three's bright pass entirely (no ceiling, one tap per texel)
- `lights=3` (slot count; `off` keeps every point light live)
- `throttle=0` (every update every frame)
- `light=v3` (v3's light rig)
- `regions=1` (the regions tinted on the ground)
- `batch=instanced|batched`
- `multidraw=0` (hide `WEBGL_multi_draw`, as Firefox does)

### v3 stage 3 switches

- `adapt=0` (hold the pixel ratio; `1` adapts even during the bench)
- `budget=<ms>` (the adaptive step's 1%-low budget; `1` forces a step down)
- `smap=` (shadow map size)
- `precompile=0` (compile each shader program at its first draw, not all of them behind the title card)
- `warmup=0` (upload each geometry and texture at its first draw, not all of them behind the title card: `src/warmup.js` draws the scene once, every object shown and unculled, into a 4×4 corner of the scene buffer with the shadow map, then `initTexture`s every material's textures; `?perf=1` logs what it uploaded and its time)
- `lean=0` (the canvas's MSAA at high and depth buffer, and depth buffers on every composer and bloom target, as before stage 3)

### v3 stage 3 vegetation and material switches (each on by default)

- `vegscan=0` (repack every plant kind whenever its chunks change, generate a chunk in one frame)
- `fargrass=0`
- `reedlod=0` (full clumps at every distance)
- `grasstier=0` (high's grass distance at every tier)
- `sort=0` (trees in build order)
- `bands=0` (terrain bump and fine detail at every distance)
- `waterfar=0` (the tarn's and fen's fine ripples at every distance)

Tree LOD (`vegetation/trees.js`): near, mid and far at 24 and 72 m (18 and 55 at low), scaled by kind, with 4 m of hysteresis. A switch closer than 120 m cross-fades for 0.4 s: one of 48 spare instances holds the old level while both dither with `fade.js` (the fade rides in the BatchedMesh's colour-texture alpha, or the instanced stand-in's `aFade`); out of spares a switch is hard (`trees.fadeOverflow`; `trees.fades` counts fades in flight).

### The v6 switches

v6-01 adds only the counters and `pop-test`. Later v6 agents append their `?param=` here, one line each.

- `post=v3` restores the three passes after the scene (bloom's full-size blend, the grade, OutputPass) in place of the finish pass (v6-02)
- `rays=full` marches the crepuscular rays at every pixel in the finish (or the grade), not in their quarter-size pass (v6-02)
- `postclock=0` pins post's clock, so the grain and the rays' jitter are the same between loads (render pairs) (v6-02)
- `bloommips=1..5` blurs only the first n of bloom's five mips (default 5; the finish chain only) (v6-02)
- `aa=off` draws without the FXAA folded into the finish pass (on at every tier) (v6-02, v6-08 Q1)
- `grain=<multiplier>` scales the film grain (0.6 = the default, the owner's pick from the v6-02 compare set; 1 = the old 0.02; 0 = none) (v6-02)
- `plantfade=0` (grass and reed chunks switch to the far clumps at once at 60 m and 50 m, instead of crossing over 8 m: a chunk within 4 m of the switch, plus 2 m slack, is packed into both meshes and the two dither by the chunk's nearest-edge distance, `src/fade.js`) (v6-05)
- `rockfade=0` (pebbles and stones cut at their reach, and fine boulders swap to their coarse twin at 140 m, instead of fading over the reach's last 15% and over 132-140 m; low: 51-60, 127.5-150, 102-110) (v6-05)
- `packstep=6` (rocks repack every mesh on one frame once the camera has moved 6 m and upload whole buffers; plant packs sort with a closure, copy with subarrays and ignore the frame's pack token, `ctx.packToken`) (v6-05)
- `xfade=0` (v6-04: trees switch LOD hard, with no spare instances, and build v5's far mesh)
- `fungifade=0` (v6-04: toadstools and pods cull by 100 m group at 90 m, with no distance fade)
- `bloomkernel=box` (v6.2: bloom's mips blur with three's near-box kernel, sigma equal to the radius and cut at it, instead of a Gaussian of the same variance taken to 3 sigma; the box is what draws a bright glint or light as nested soft squares)
- `bloomprefilter=0` (v6.2: bloom's bright pass reads the scene with one bilinear tap per texel, 2×2 of the 4×4 pixels under it, instead of four taps over all of them; one tap turns a line one pixel wide into dots). `?bloomkernel=box&bloomprefilter=0` is v6's bloom

### v9 music

- `music=<track>`: plays that track (any of the nine) 3 s after the walk begins, whatever the setting, and nothing else (the director is out of the load); `music=0` is no music. Unknown names warn in the console.

The setting "Music (experimental): off / rare / default / frequent" is in the settings panel's Sound group (`fp-music` in localStorage), **off by default** (v9-M6): with it off nothing of the music but its glue is fetched, built or ticked. The debug panel lists the tracks as rows (playing one loads the music).

### v13

- `serial=1` (v13-P0): every world module imported first, then built one at a time, so each build's time is its own (`src/startup.js`); for timing only (`tools/load-probe.mjs`), never for a visitor or a render: modules then build in `MODULES` order to completion, and at the spawn on low its frame differs from an ordinary load's by 85 pixels on one figure (two ordinary loads: 0)
- `sw=0` (v13-L1): unregisters the service worker (`sw.js`), deletes its caches and keeps it off in this browser (`localStorage['fp-sw']`); `sw=1` turns it back on, and is the only way it registers on localhost (it needs a server that stamps `X-Fp-Commit` and serves `commit.json`: `node tools/sw-site.mjs <built _site> <port>`). With no `?sw=1` on localhost nothing registers, so every test and `tools/serve.sh` run without it. `fp-swr=1|2` marks a reload the worker caused (removed at once; at most one per page, a second turns the worker off until a new commit). `node tools/sw-test.mjs [port]` (in the suite as `sw`, about 25 s; `FP_BROWSER=firefox|webkit` for those engines, which skip the two CDP steps) proves it on a small site of its own. The worker's log: post `{type:'fp-trace'}` with a MessageChannel to `navigator.serviceWorker.controller`. A dedicated Worker (`new Worker(url, {type:'module'})`, no query string in `url`: the service worker keeps nothing with one) is bound to its page's release through its script's fetch, in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit alike; so its first visit's imports are kept too, have the Worker post `performance.getEntriesByType('resource').map((e) => e.name)` to the page and push them onto `window.__fp_fill_extra` before the walk is ready
- `bakeslope=mesh` (v13-L2, a trial, off): the terrain bake takes the biome's slope from the mesh's heights a cell either side, not `slopeAt`'s four `heightAt` at 0.6 m; not the same slope, so the colours move (L2's report has the counts); for pair renders, never a default
- `worker=0` (v13-L3): the terrain's bake on the page, as before v13, not in its worker; `kept=0`: nothing read from or written to the store; `bakehold=<ms>` holds the bake back that long (a test: a module that reads the mesh without `{ mesh: true }` then fails loudly). All three are for tests and before/after runs

**The bake and the store** (v13-L3):

- `src/v13/bake.js` makes the terrain mesh at once (`terrain.js` `makeTerrain`: x and z, material, in the scene; its geometry's `attributes` throw until baked) and takes the bake from whichever answers first: the store, or `src/height/bake-worker.js` (a module worker on a URL with no query, started at once; the page's query goes in its first message as `FP_PAGE_QUERY`; it traces the leads, posts them with its height params' key, bakes with `src/height/bake.js`, transfers the arrays, keeps them), or the page (no Worker, `?worker=0`, a failed worker, params that differ). All three give the same bits.
- Only the leads are awaited before the scaffold (every `heightAt` after them has the runnels' cuts; nothing may sample the height earlier).
- A module that reads the mesh's heights or colours, or calls a sampler that does (`vegetation/ground.js`, `v4/regions/common.js` `groundOf`, `skin.js` `meshGround`, `fauna/uncanny/kit.js` `makeGround`, `water/river.js`), is marked `M` (`{ mesh: true }`) in `MODULES` and builds after the bake, each in a task of its own; the rest build beside it. The material and `geometry.parameters` are there from the start. `ctx.terrain.ready` is a promise of the baked terrain. A module that fails is named on the title card's error line and in `startup`'s `extra.failed`; `?bakehold=20000` finds an unmarked reader (swept on low, medium and high: none).
- `startup`'s `extra.bake`: `source` (kept, worker, page: where the bake used came from), `fellBack` (why the page baked), `leadsMs`, `bakedMs`, `storeMs` from the bake's start, the worker's own `workerLeadsMs`, `workerBakeMs`, and `kept`.
- The store (`src/v13/kept.js`): IndexedDB `fp-kept`, a record under `<tree>|<slot>` (`bake:<segs>`, `kept:<name>:<tier>`) with its key (the tier and every URL switch but the view-only ones, `queryKey`) and a checksum. The tree is what ran: off localhost (or with `?kept=commit` on it), `commit:<sha>` only when the `X-Fp-Commit` stamps of `kept.js`'s and `bake.js`'s own responses (force-cache) equal `commit.json`'s commit, else the store is off for the load (no read, write or prune); on localhost, `src`: the record lists every script its context loaded with a hash of its text and holds while they still hash the same. A put replaces its slot; `prune()` (after ready) deletes other trees' keys without reading a record. Any miss, error or 2.5 s timeout computes.
- `ctx.kept(name, compute)`: a module's own plain data and typed arrays in the same store, snapshotted when computed and written after ready, one put a `requestIdleCallback` (Safari has none: a `setTimeout(0)`, one put a task); written at once they cost the cold load ~100 ms on low (v13 L4). `keptStats.putMs` gives each put's main-thread ms (checksum and clone); the bench (`bench-report`) prints them. `ctx.kept.preload()` (main.js starts it with the store, each build awaits it after its import) reads and checks this tier's records before any build, so a build's own `kept`/`open` waits a microtask, never IndexedDB, and the store's timing does not move the order modules register updates and colliders in. `rocks.place` and `thrones.mesh` keep a whole compute; `ctx.kept.open(name)` gives the slot itself (`{ data }` on a hit, `keep(v)` on a miss) for data whole only later (`plinths.feet`: emitFigure skipped, every draw and collider still made). Before converting, list what the compute touches besides its result (a shared rng, clearances, colliders, ctx fields): it must stay outside the kept part.
- The height tape (v13 L4, `src/v13/tape.js`): `const tape = await keptHeights(ctx, name)`, then `tape.run(() => …)` round each synchronous stretch of the build (no await inside; for that stretch `ctx.terrain`'s heightAt, slopeAt, causewayDistance and biomeAt are the tape's, so helpers that read `ctx.terrain` are taped too) or `tape.terrain` for a module that only asks its own copy, and `tape.done()` at the end. A first visit records every call, one slot a query (`<name>.heightAt`, `.slopeAt`, `.causewayDistance`, `.biomeAt`) in parts of at most 1.5 MB (`.heightAt.1`, ...: no put holds the main thread over ~6 ms at 1x); a repeat answers a call from the tape only when (x, z) match the next entry, or one of the 8 after it (it resyncs past calls the module no longer makes), bit for bit, else from the terrain without moving on, so the module's code runs unchanged and every answer is the same number. Taped: vegetation, galleries, region-adust, annals, water, inhabitants2 (low ~12 MB, medium ~15 MB of tape). `keptStats.timing[i].tape` (on each module's `.heightAt` slot): calls, fromTape, missed, skipped (both 0 when the code is unchanged), bytes. Not worth it where the queries are cheap (structures: 8 ms). `ctx.built(name)`: a promise of that module's build settling (true built, false failed or not loaded); await it in a build only for a module earlier in `MODULES`; `ctx.built()` is every module's, for a `.then`, never an await in a build.
- Checks: `tools/loading-test.mjs` (in the suite: keep, hit, a new key replaces, prune, spoiled records, the switches, stamped records and a stamp/`commit.json` mismatch on a site served as Netlify serves it, no module failed; v13 L4: every tape answered on a reload, and a tape out of the module's order, a call changed, dropped and added, answered by the terrain there with the Galleries built vertex for vertex the same). Not in the suite, for a change to the bake, the store or what a kept module builds: `node tools/height-check.mjs purebake <rec>` (the worker's bake bit for bit; ~2 min plus a recording), `node tools/build-check.mjs <B> --repeat` (a reload built from the store; it fails unless every `ctx.kept` record hit and every tape answered all its calls, and prints each one's counts) and `--throttle=4|6` (a build-order race shows as a MOVED), `node tools/bake-sw-check.mjs` (the bake worker under `sw.js` on a stamped site; `FP_BROWSER=firefox|webkit`).

**Under the ground** (v14-D0, `src/v14/under.js`): how the next room is made dark, sealed and stone-sounding.

- **Sealed:** the terrain is single-sided and casts no shadow, so a room under it is closed by its own meshes, drawn to face in (floor, walls, vault, the far end; a passage's end a black quad). Open the ground over it as the Nave does (its terrain triangles made degenerate after the bake; never `heightAt`) or not at all.
- **Below:** `const below = installBelow(ctx); const h = below.add(fn, { size })`. `fn(x, y, z) → 0..1`: `boxBelow(x0, x1, z0, z1, top, deep, edge)` (a hall: `top` a number or `(x, z) → y`) or `pathBelow(ctx.terrain, [[x, z], ...], { half, cover, deep, edge, top })` (a passage: within `half` of the line, below `heightAt - cover` sampled every 2 m along it, so a 155 m diagonal is under all the way). `size`: 0 a narrow passage .. 1 a hall (the stone room's tail, `audio/graph.js`). `h()` removes it; `h.fn` is the volume, to reuse where volumes meet (`below.inside` is the largest of them, so a passage whose line runs a metre into the hall's box never dips at the mouth). `below.inside(x, y, z)` is stone in the audio (pass the listener's `y`: `space.at(x, z, out, y)`; no `y` means the surface), shelters the wind, gives the hard step; `below.here` (the camera's, set by `post.js` each frame) takes the sun's glow off screen and eases the grade to `UNDER_GRADE` (the dark-adapted eye: navy, not black, on a phone).
- **The seal:** while `below.here` is 1, the camera draws only layer `LAYER_BELOW`: what was `below.keep(obj)`'d (the place's own meshes: keep every mesh you build down there, or it is not drawn), every light the camera saw, its own children, anything with `frustumCulled: false` that is not an instanced set, and, per drawable, what lies in the cone from the eye through an opening (`below.opening({ x, y, z, r })`, a level disc: the Nave's hole) or casts a shadow within 110 m of one (three draws the sun's shadow map by the main camera's layers, and the low sun's shadows fall in through the hole). Self-culling instanced sets (far.js's) are judged by their instances' sphere, refreshed two a frame. `?seal=0` turns it off. Measured on low at the Nave's floor: see D0's report (looking back at the hole from the east end: 285 → 140 draw calls, JS a frame 4.3–6.2 → 2.0–2.9 ms, the rim 3.2–5.1; looking down the hall 112 → 98). A thing added to the scene after the first descent is drawn under the seal only through an opening: keep it.
- **Dark:** build the room with `UnderBuilder(lit)` (one mesh of flat parts, vertex colours) and `underMaterial(new MeshStandardMaterial({ vertexColors: true }), key, glow, { far: [near, far] })`. `lit(x, y, z, out)` writes `aUnder` per vertex: `[0]` the sun and the sky's light (1 on the moor, ~0.01 deep under a roof), `[1]` the haze (0 in a sealed room), `[2]` a baked glow of the room's own lamps in `glow`'s colour (`v5/nave.js` `glowAt` is the model). `far` fades the whole fragment to black between `near` and `far` m from the eye, so a passage ends in dark, not on a lit wall. Point lights and emissive pass untouched: add `PointLight`s with a real `distance` (lights.js keeps the nearest three).
- **Anything else down there** (a creature, a prop, another module's mesh): `belowLit(material)` on a built-in lit material cuts its sky light and haze by the registered volumes, per fragment from its world position (up to `MAX_SEG` = 16 capsules, shared uniforms: boxes give one, a path one per 20 m). Its limit: ShaderMaterials, Points, Sprites and unlit materials (the act's lure, particles) are not cut, and a material compiled before a volume is added still sees it (uniforms), but one made after 16 capsules ignores the rest.
- **Floor:** one `ctx.addWalkable(fn, { stack: true, keep: true, name })` for everything walked down there (stairs as a slope: treads are drawn, not walked), and a catch under any drop (the Nave: a held stacked floor in its water and a 0.9 s rescue back onto the road). Register the way down with `ctx.ascents.add({ held: true, ... })`; `tools/edges-test.mjs` has the Coffin Road's side, hop and water trials to copy.

A passage, in ten lines (from the Nave's south door toward the Pit):

```js
import { installBelow, pathBelow, underMaterial, UnderBuilder } from '../v14/under.js';
const line = [[246.7, 54], [240, 80], [215, 160], [202, 182]];            // the floor's line
const below = installBelow(ctx);
below.add(pathBelow(ctx.terrain, line, { half: 1.6, cover: 2, deep: 2 }), { size: 0.3 }); // under, and it sounds narrow
const lit = (x, y, z, out) => { out[0] = 0.01; out[1] = 0; out[2] = lampGlow(x, y, z); return out; };
const B = new UnderBuilder(lit); /* walls, vault, floor, end caps facing in */
const mesh = new THREE.Mesh(B.geometry(), underMaterial(new THREE.MeshStandardMaterial({ vertexColors: true }), 'passage', '#9fe8c2', { far: [12, 30] }));
ctx.scene.add(mesh); below.keep(mesh);                                      // drawn under the seal
ctx.addWalkable(floorAlong(line), { stack: true, keep: true, name: 'passage' });
for (const p of lampsAlong(line, 9)) ctx.scene.add(lampLight(p));           // a next light every 9 m
```

## Quality tiers

Quality tiers (`src/tier.js`). Without `q=`, the tier pinned in the debug panel's quality rows (kept in localStorage), else the device: a phone or 4 cores or fewer is low; a discrete GPU (by its WebGL renderer name, or WebGPU's adapter info) high; an integrated one medium; a software renderer low; anything else (Apple silicon, a masked name) starts high and drops to medium if the first 3 s of full-rate frames average over 1.15 frame intervals. Medium builds what high builds; it differs only in this table, so high and medium switch live and low reloads:

| tier | pixel ratio | adaptive floor | shadow map | 1%-low budget | builds as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| high | 1.25 | 1.0 | 4096² | 20 ms | high |
| medium | 1.0 | 0.75 | 2048² | 33 ms | high |
| low | 0.85 | 0.6 | 2048² | 33 ms | low |

The pixel ratio is never above the display's, and never above 1.0 at medium or high on a GPU without its own memory (an integrated GPU or Apple silicon, by its renderer name; the floors stay). The adaptive ratio measures steady frames: the view turning under 0.35 rad/s and moving under 4.5 m/s for 250 ms. When their 1% low has been over the budget for 5 s of steady time (turning pauses the count), the ratio steps down 0.1 toward the floor; if the view has not held still for 20 s and every frame's 1% low has been over budget all that time, it steps instead. After 45 s with every frame's 1% low under 0.6 of the budget it steps back up 0.05, never to a ratio that failed in the last minute. After a step, a tier change or the tier probe, nothing steps for 20 s. Stalls (over 50 ms and twice the running mean) are left out. A due step waits for a steady frame right after a shadow redraw, so the composer's reallocation never lands mid-turn or on a redraw; `report().tier.steps` records each step's reason and wait. `?dpr=` sets the ratio and holds it.

Shadow cadence (`src/shadows.js`): the map is redrawn on a 2 Hz clock, at once when a creature within 40 m moves a texel, and when the box (40 m ahead of you, texel-snapped) has moved 3 m, which turning does constantly. While the view turns (over 0.35 rad/s), those box moves come at most 15 times a second (`shadowdyn=`), 10 by default on a GPU without its own memory, and only after a frame within the tier's budget; after an over-budget frame the redraw waits a frame, never more than 0.12 s (8 a second). Walking moves the box at once. Texel snapping keeps the shadows still between redraws (`shots/wip/fix-dpr-shadow-cadence-*`).

## The profiler

Measuring (`src/perf.js`): `?perf=1`, or the debug panel's `profiler` row, shows an overlay top-left with frame time (2 s rolling average and 1% low, the mean of the slowest 1% of frames), CPU ms per module inside the update loop (`frame` is main.js's own work, `core` updates registered outside a module), GPU ms per pass (render including the shadow map, the shadow map alone, bloom, rays, finish, and `post`, their sum; `grade` and `output` with `?post=v3`, null otherwise) where the browser has `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` and `n/a` where it does not, draw calls, triangles, geometry, texture and program counts, lights by type (visible/total), pixel ratio and render size. `?bench=1` (45 s) or `?bench=<seconds>` is the one representative run (v13, `src/perf.js` and `src/perf-frames.js`): the round (spawn, the causeway, the tarn's shore, the fen's edge, home; `summary` is this alone, as every bench before v13 reported it), then two legs a visitor makes, each a teleport and 15% of the round's time standing (1.5 to 8 s): `top` (the settings map's Go to, the Span's deck at 56 m) and `wake` (`ctx.fall.wake()`: the fall's own waking at the keep's gate, lids, look and release). Controls off, the gutter held. It keeps every frame's JS (frameBegin to frameEnd: the number that is trustworthy headless) and reports, per leg and over all, the median, 95th, 99th, worst and counts over 2× and 3× the median (`legs`, `js`); `hitches`: every frame over 50 ms or 3× the run's median, the worst sixty, each with its leg, place, largest row and callback, and what else happened (`shadow`, `plants`, `rocks`, `far`, `texFirst`, `texData`, `geometry`, `program`, `buffer`, `tier`, `teleport`, `station`), and `worstTen`, the ten slowest frames whatever the rule (so a run with no hitch still names its worst); `unattributed` (what no row names: about 1% of all JS on low); `memory` at the start and end (the JS heap from `performance.memory` in Chromium, rounded unless the browser runs with `--enable-precise-memory-info`; `measureUserAgentSpecificMemory` only on a cross-origin-isolated page, which this site is not; nothing in Firefox or Safari; geometry bytes per module; gpumem's bytes); `draws`: calls and triangles per module, colour and shadow passes apart, at seven stations (five along the round, one in each leg; `other` is the post chain): the hooks go on every drawable at a station and come off when it has its colour and shadow pass, and those frames (`station`, about 1–15 each) are left out of the distributions, `stationFrames` says how many. `summary`'s avg and 1% low compare with pre-v13 reports; its cpuMs splits the old `frame` row (`--against` compares a pre-v13 file only on what means the same, and warns when tier, duration, throttle, dpr, size or cap differ). After a Measure run, Close reloads the clean URL (`q` kept only if it was there before), so nothing stays hooked. `--throttle=4` applies Chromium's CPU throttle from the page's ready, a stand-in for a phone. It ends on a panel (the summary, Copy: the short JSON, about 9 KB, `__fp.perf.brief()`; Download: everything). The settings panel's Measure row reloads into `?bench=45&perf=1&measure=1` at the tier in use: the way to get numbers off a phone. `node tools/bench-report.mjs "<url>?bench=45&perf=1&q=low" out.json` runs it headlessly, saves the report and prints the panel's lines; `node tools/bench-report.mjs a.json --against=b.json` prints a saved one and diffs every number the two share (a pre-v13 report diffs on the round's summary). The profiler's rows (v13): beside each module, the costs no module owns, each timed where the call is made and taken out of the row around it: `render` (the composer's render: three's traversal, sorting and GL calls, less what is named inside it), `shadow.pass` (the shadow map's CPU on the frames it is redrawn), `far` (`far.js`'s packing; it counts `far.instances`), `controls`, `sky`, `hud`, `post.update`, `shadow.cadence`, `lights.slots`, `bench.drive`, `perf` (the profiler's own); `frame` is what is left of main.js's own work. These wraps, and the GPU timer queries, go in at the first frame the profiler is on (`perf.hooked`), never with `?perf` absent; gpumem also counts buffer uploads. `?record=<seconds>` (it implies `?perf=1`; `__fp.perf.record(seconds)` starts one from the console) records a pan: once the walker is out (card hidden, pointer locked, drag-look or touch) it keeps every frame's duration, timestamp and view yaw, and every 250 ms a sample (GPU passes, draw calls, triangles, pixel ratio and buffer, tier steps, shadow redraws total and dynamic, camera position and yaw, geometry, texture and program counts, uploads, CPU per module), skips idle time, and at the end releases the pointer lock and shows the bench's panel with the median, 95th, 99th and worst frame, the counts over 2× and 3× the median, the worst ten with the sample around each, and the whole record to download as `record-<date>-<commit>-<quality>-<machine>.json` (`__fp.perf.lastRecord()` returns it). The owner types `?q=medium&record=10`, clicks Go out, pans for ten seconds and sends the file. `__fp.perf.report()` returns everything as a plain object; the owner's bench files go in `shots/perf/`. The debug panel's `measure` rows switch each world module (its scene objects hidden, its updates skipped; objects it parents elsewhere, colliders and running sounds stay), bloom and shadows on and off at runtime. `node tools/perf-test.mjs` runs a short bench headlessly and checks the report against `renderer.info`, the toggles and the `n/a` path. `node tools/record-test.mjs ["<url>?q=medium&record=6&pos=-190,165"] [shot.png]` clicks Go out, turns for the recording and checks the record (frame count, samples, GPU rows or `n/a`, worst ten sorted with yaws, the file name). `node tools/pan-test.mjs "<url>?q=medium&perf=1&adapt=0" [max]` runs two stations in one browser: A at (−190, 165) stands still 4 s, then turns the view 360° twice over 6 s each; B in the Deodand Wood at (−195, 128) stands still, then turns from north at 1.2 rad/s to yaw 3.9, and reports yaw 3.5–3.9 apart, where the Great Span and its kit enter the view. It prints the worst frames with each one's modules, shadow redraw and new programs, geometries and textures; it fails if a shader program compiles or a geometry or texture uploads while turning (`renderer.info` before and after the turn), or a turning frame's CPU passes `max` (default 3) times the still median. With `pos=` in the url it runs that spot alone as station A.

`node tools/pop-test.mjs [base] [--label=before] [--only=uploads,trees,...] [--q=medium]` (v6) counts what pops, swaps and uploads and measures pops in pictures, in one browser, with `perf=1&adapt=0&wind=0`. The counters: `ctx.perf.count(key, n = 1)` (one boolean test when the profiler is off, allocation-free after a key's first use), `report().counters` (`totals`, `lastFrame`), each 250 ms record sample's `counters` (deltas); keys `trees.lodSwitches`, `fungi.groupFlips`, `plants.repacks`, `plants.farSwitches`, `rocks.packs`, `shadow.redraws.move|clock|dynamic|turn`, `lights.handovers`, `tier.steps`, `uploads.programs|geometries|textures` and `uploads.texFirst|texData` (gpumem's uploads split by whether the texture had been uploaded before).

The profiler also times each update callback on its own, labelled by the file:line that registered it (`report().cpu.callbacks`, the bench's `cpuCallbacks`, and `report().firstBlock` for the first half second). A bench run names its machine (`?machine=`, remembered per browser) and, on the deployed site or the artifact, its commit (`commit.json`, written by the Netlify build and `tools/build-artifact.mjs`). `node tools/alloc-audit.mjs "<url>?bench=30&q=medium" out.json` runs the bench (or, with no `bench=` and `&idle=0`, the walker standing at the spawn: what the profiler costs when it is off) and counts, per rendered frame, the THREE math objects `src/` constructs (by file:line and caller, through a counting wrapper of `three`) and every JS allocation by the `src/` function that made it (V8's sampling heap profiler). In per-frame code use `hyp(x, z)` from `noise.js`, not `Math.hypot`: V8's `Math.hypot` allocates on every call and is ~20× slower.

The profiler's `gpu memory` line counts the bytes held by textures, renderbuffers and the canvas, and texture uploads per frame (`src/gpumem.js`; `report().memory.gpu`). Its GL hooks go in only with `?perf=1` or `?bench`, or when the profiler row is turned on, in which case it counts only what is allocated from then on. `report().memory` is the one memory object: the counts, `gpu` (these bytes, and buffer uploads), `geometry` (attribute and index bytes per module) and `jsHeap`.

The load (v13-P0, `src/startup.js`): a mark at each boundary from navigation to "Go out" (`performance.getEntriesByType('measure')`, named `fp:<phase>`, so they show in a browser's profiler), the main-thread tasks over 50 ms with their phase, and each module's import and build ms; `window.__fp_startup.report()`, and `startup` in `perf.report()` and the bench's JSON. `node tools/load-probe.mjs <base> [--q=low] [--runs=3] [--throttle=4] [--net=fast4g] [--json=path]` does a cold load, a reload and a `?serial=1` load per run in a fresh context and prints one table: each phase's median [min–max], time to ready, the longest stall, requests, KB and cache hits, and the ten slowest module builds. A phase is named for the mark that ends it; in an ordinary load `imports` waits behind the builds, so read the modules' cost from the serial column. The local server has no latency, compression or HTTP/2 and sends `no-store`: the network's truth is the live site, read-only, by hand (`node tools/load-probe.mjs https://below-a-fading-star.netlify.app --runs=3`; a tree from before v13 gives ready, the stalls and the network, no phases).
`node tools/load-probe.mjs <base> --profile [--q=low] [--interval=200] [--inclusive=a,b] [--out=path]` (v13-P2, `tools/load-profile.mjs`) profiles where the CPU goes: a cold load and a `?serial=1` load under CDP's sampling Profiler, then an unprofiled `?serial=1` load with `src/height/field.js` (the heightfield) and `src/noise.js` rewritten in flight (page.route; nothing in src/ changes) to count heightAt, normalAt, slopeAt, biomeAt, noise2 and heightAt's exact repeats. It prints the top 40 functions and top 25 files by self time, inclusive time for named functions, each phase's top files, what inside heightAt costs, and each module build's heightAt share and call count; raw `.cpuprofile`s go to `shots/wip/`.

`node tools/height-check.mjs [record|check|bench|pure] [file] [--params=mountains=v4] [--quick]` (v13-L2, node only, no browser) is the height path bit for bit: it imports `src/terrain.js` in node (three from `vendor/`, a stub location), bakes low and medium, and samples heightAt (exact and under each tier's coarse pass), `WALL_SAMPLE`, slopeAt, normalAt, biomeAt, regionAt (its own object, and the air's blend into a caller's), waterLevelAt, causewayDistance and riverAt at about 1.3 million fixed points (both bakes' vertices, grids, the causeway and its bed's edges, the leads, the regions' outlines and both boxes' edges, the landmarks' blocks at their reach, r 284 and 352, z -200, the shores, the river, the Span, the icefall, the wall), every value raw, plus every baked attribute; `check` compares them with a recording element for element by their bits (a PASS/FAIL per group, the first differing point and its values, a sha1 per group as summary; for a bake that differs, the count over 1/255) and asserts `|noise2| < 1.1`. The recording's header carries the commit it was made at and whether `src/` was clean; `check` prints both and fails a recording made from a dirty `src/`. `pure` imports `src/height/field.js` as a worker would (three refused, no location, document or window) and checks its queries at the recording's points; `bench` gives µs a call and the node bake's ms; `--quick` about a fifth of the points, low only. `node tools/terrain-probe.mjs <base> [q] [runs] [out.json]` times `buildTerrain` in a bare page (no walk) and fingerprints what it builds (heightAt at 5,000 wall samples, a checksum of each baked attribute); `--diff a.json b.json` compares two trees, for a change to the terrain that must not change what is seen.
`node tools/build-check.mjs <base> [--q=low] [--params=a=1] [--runs=1] [--record=a.json] [--against=a.json] [--only=a,b] [--allowunstable]` (v13-L5) fingerprints what every module built, for a change to a builder that must not change what is seen: at the `ready` mark, before the first frame, it hashes bit for bit each module's objects (the profiler's tagging; untagged is `core`): geometry attributes and indices with their types, instance matrices, colours and counts, world matrices and mesh flags, material scalars, defines and uniforms, texture pixels and sampling parameters. A last line, `colliders` (v13 L4), hashes every collider's fields bit for bit, the set in any order. It does not see shader code changed in `onBeforeCompile`, walkables, registered updates, acts, or anything outside the scene. `--against` needs a recording with the same `q` and `params`, prints IDENTICAL or MOVED per module (with the entries that moved), and exits 1 on any MOVED or when nothing was compared. Record the tree before your change with `--runs=3`, against a server of that tree (the tool serves nothing: serve a copy yourself, e.g. `git worktree add` or `git archive HEAD | tar -x -C <dir>`, and `http-server -p <port>` there). Entries that differ between those loads are recorded as unstable and skipped. An entry unstable in a later run but stable in the recording is a MOVED, and a module with over 10% of its entries unstable is a MOVED unless `--allowunstable`. Two are known to race: fauna's cloak takes its first spot from whether structures has built yet (it moves there 2 s into the walk anyway), and region-bletting builds without vegetation's trees if they are not built within 15 s of its start (a loaded machine, medium). A load takes 10 to 20 s.
