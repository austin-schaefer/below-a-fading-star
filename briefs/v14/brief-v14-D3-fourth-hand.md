## The orchestrator's direction (a starting point with a spine; make it stranger, not tamer; where the owner's words and this differ, theirs win)

- **Who and when.** Between the Heliarchy and the folk here now. The Heliarchs faced the sun and built to it; when it began to fail, the people after them stopped facing it and tried to **keep** it: to gather daylight while there was daylight, store it, and send it where it was wanted. They are the makers of the tubes. A name in the valley's voice is yours to find (three candidates in your report, one chosen; mine, as a placeholder: the Meridiate [Proposed]).
- **The hand.** Nothing carved, coursed, jointed or nailed; nothing of stone, timber or iron. Everything is **made in one piece**: blown, cast, spun, extruded, grown: glass, glaze, fired ceramic, a milky resin (the owner's plastisteel). **Round in section**: tubes, ribs, bulbs, nodes, collars, lenses, beads; swellings where a line was joined, like kelp bladders or galls. Their everyday motif is **the ring** (the Elder's is the triangle, the Heliarchy's the hexagon, the Late folk's the crescent). Colour: milk white, bottle amber, celadon, a band of ox-blood glaze where a thing was marked, all of it now crazed, clouded, chipped, silted, barnacled with lime; and their one unnatural light, **stored daylight**: the cool true white-gold of a noon nobody alive has seen (the Speculum's pool is the one daylight-blue sky in the world, a deliberate exception: this is its cousin, and as rare). At a scale that is theirs: a tube a visitor walks inside, a vessel taller than they are.
- **The road.** One of their lines ran through the Heliarchs' drowned nave, and broke. What a visitor walks on is what it carried: a string of great sealed vessels (their carriers, each once filled with a measure of noon), still coupled nose to tail, afloat across the black water from the stair's foot to the east wall, riding low, some rolled, some cracked and full of water, most dark, **a few still holding their light**, which is the light the hall is seen by (no candles: nothing here is lit by a flame unless a later person brought it). The broken tube itself should be seen: its torn mouth in a wall or the roof, a length of it fallen across the columns, ribs of it under the water. Not the first idea: a visitor should feel they are walking on the stalled freight of a people who tried to bank the sun.
- **Who uses it now** (each uses the last: `architecture.md`, spolia). The timber shears and the hung stair are the Late folk's, as they are; they are somebody's who comes down for the light. The library has a loose end made for it: the Leerie and the tender light the valley's lamps, with "the same lamp oil?" and nobody knows from where (`stories/README.md`; `stories/the-tender-of-the-lattices.md`; the Leerie's flask on his slate). One carrier near the stair has been tapped; what stands on the trestle at the head, where the coffin was, is theirs. Say it without a tally.
- **The story.** Rewrite `stories/the-coffin-road.md` under the road's new name (and rename the place card, the settings stand, `ctx` names a visitor never sees may stay). The Sexton's forty other graves go back to being an open thread in the index. The Reckoner's "Two floors, or one?" and the picked gold of the east wall's sun stay.
