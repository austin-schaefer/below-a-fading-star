# Brief v15-Q1: the Quarrels (the crown: the masons, the new house, the upper square)

Worktree `.claude/worktrees/issue-1510-quarrels`, branch `issue-1510-quarrels`, port 8161. Work only there. Read `briefs/v15/_quarter.md` first and do what it says. Module: `src/v15/quarters/quarrels.js`.

## Goal

The top of the village, on the level ground under the keep's fallen north-east corner: the only place in the valley where anything is being built. The Late folk are quarrying Carcanet Keep itself: its fallen blocks, each still carrying a piece of some Heliarch ornament they cannot read or copy, are being dragged, split and re-laid as the walls of a house that is half up. A visitor coming along the old causeway, or up the Heliarch stair from below, arrives in a working yard and sees new stone for the first time, and sees that it is the oldest stone anyone could reach.

Yours: the plots the plan gives `quarrels` (the masons' yard, the new house, `quarrels-house-*`, the square), the stair's head court, and the fallen corner itself as a quarry face.

Set pieces, bend them if you find better:
1. **The quarry in the keep's wound.** The fallen corner worked as a face: wedges in a split block, a block half lifted on a shears and windlass of raw timber, a sledge on rollers on a track of planks leading to the yard, drag marks. The masons take only fallen stone: a chalk line on the standing wall shows where someone has thought about taking more.
2. **The new house**, the kit's `new` at its largest and most particular: three courses up on one side and to the lintel on the other, a centring under an arch that is not yet turned, a lime pit and a lime kiln (lit: its glow and smoke are the crown's beacon from the road), the templates hung on a wall: wooden profiles cut to match mouldings, none of which match.
3. **The upper square** (`quarrels-square`): paved in whatever came to hand (Heliarch hexagons, a keep slab, cobble), a cistern head or trough fed by the last arch of the aqueduct from the keep's hill (`src/structures/scatter.js` has the ruined aqueduct: join to it or end it here, dry or dripping), a bench against a warm wall, and the long view down the stair's notch onto the Pomegranate House kept clear.
4. One or two Late houses of masons, built of the same robbed stone at the foot and plaster above.

No vessel form here. Nothing medieval (no cranes with treadwheels, no banners).

## Done when

- From the causeway west of the village and from the stair's foot looking up, the crown reads as a building site against the keep, by a lit kiln and one or two lamps.
- A visitor walks in from the causeway, round the yard, into the new house's shell (walkable, with the scaffold's lowest stage climbable by a ladder from the ways kit if it can be made safe), and down the stair.
- As `_quarter.md` says: zero geometry findings, the route frames looked at one by one, the tests, the counts.
