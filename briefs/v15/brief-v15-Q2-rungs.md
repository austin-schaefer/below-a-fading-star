# Brief v15-Q2: the Rungs (the three steps: the stacked houses)

Worktree `.claude/worktrees/issue-1511-rungs`, branch `issue-1511-rungs`, port 8162. Work only there. Read `briefs/v15/_quarter.md` first and do what it says. Module: `src/v15/quarters/rungs.js`.

## Goal

The village's body: the fourteen or so house plots on the Mid and Low Rungs and the High Rung's edge (`rungs-*` in the plan), stacked so that one house's roof is the next one's yard, and threaded with ways. This is where the owner's "stairs, maybe even ladders, and other verticality" lives. A visitor should be able to leave the lane, climb an outside stair to a roof, cross that roof and a plank to the next, go up a ladder to the tread above, look down on the lane they were in, and come down another way: and lose count of how.

The ways that exist (V0's: the Heliarch stair, the daylight main, one ladder, one bridge to the aqueduct's pier, a plank stair) are the spine; you add the capillaries with the ways kit (`src/v15/ways/`: `roofWalk`, the ladder, plank stairs, bridges) and the houses' own roofs and stairs.

Set pieces, bend them if you find better:
1. **The three stairs of three ages, side by side**, seen from one spot on the Mid Rung: the Heliarch stair (exact, broken, patched), the main (walked inside), and a Late way of your own beside them that is the meanest and the only whole one. Give the spot a reason to stand there (a bench, a wall to lean on).
2. **A roof walk of at least four roofs** on the Low Rung, joined by planks and a step or two, with what roofs are for: beds for hot nights, washing, drying trays, a dovecote, pots, a loom frame.
3. **The dyers' stair** [Proposed]: one outside stair on the east run hung with cloth drying down its length, saffron, cream, madder, a vat at its foot, runnels of colour down the paving into the drain. (A woman who waited on the Span has a room at its top: batch 3 furnishes it; leave the window and the room.)
4. **A lane under**: where the lane ducks under the main and the pier, make it a place (a niche, a lamp, a door).
5. One vessel form only: a **sherd**, a third of a vast broken vessel standing as an apse with a plaster house built into it, where the main's line was first laid (near its head).

Late plaster carries it; two or three plots are Elder slab-houses or Heliarch undercrofts where the plan says. Every house `against` a riser meets the tread above with its roof: no slots.

## Done when

- The roof walk, the three stairs' spot and at least three routes between each pair of adjoining levels are walked on keys and touch; every ascent registered; no drop over the guard without a parapet; nowhere to be stuck (hop and scramble onto every roof edge you can reach and say what happened).
- From the Lees looking up, and from the crown looking down, it reads as a hillside of houses with lit windows, not a wall with boxes on it.
- As `_quarter.md` says: zero geometry findings, the route frames looked at one by one, the tests, the counts (55k triangles at low for this quarter).
