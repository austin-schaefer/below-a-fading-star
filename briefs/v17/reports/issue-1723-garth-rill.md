# issue-1723: the Garth's spout (the owner's "white card")

**What it was.** At the rill's head north of the door (bearing `INLET`, wall point -27.2, 311.8), `water.js` drew the spout as three bare faces: a sloping underside of a "bronze lip" (the tilted dark quad), a flat back plate on the wall (the upright dark quad) and two thin cheeks, with no top and no thickness. The fall under it was a 0.22 m by 1.1 m ribbon in the water mesh's opaque shader, coloured `mix(dark, uNoonCol * 0.18..0.48, 0.6)`: a flat, opaque, near-white card standing on the rill's end. Its streaks scrolled, but a rectangle with hard edges doesn't read as water. The comment promised a basin, but there wasn't one. Reproduced at (-26, 316), yaw 0, pitch -0.18, medium (`shots/wip/garth-rill-before-*.png`).

**What it is now.** `spout(B, b)` in `garth/water.js`, called for every bearing in `SPOUTS` (plan.js; there is one: the hall has one inlet, and the water leaves by the grate at `DRAIN`, which is bars with thickness and was left alone). It is merged into the hall's builder, so it adds no draw. A coursed block in the plinth's darker stone stands 0.07 m proud of the plinth, floor to 1.6 m, bedded 0.25 m into the drum, with a paler moulded cap and a dark shadow course under it. A dark slot is cut in its upper course. A paler stone trough with two cheeks, on a corbel, runs out of the slot and has a dark wet floor. A splash stain runs down the face. Every piece is a closed box or a face laid on stone.

The fall: two crossed ribbons along a ballistic arc (0.5 m/s out, about 0.24 m of throw). Each is 0.12 m wide at the lip, narrowing, then spreading where it lands. The shader has soft edges, strands sliding down, a brighter core, and is see-through (alpha about 0.3 to 0.8). Where it lands, the rill shader draws a boil and rings (`uFall`). The water material is now transparent with depthWrite off and renderOrder -1; the rills stay at alpha 1. The rill from the spout now starts at the block's face. Draw calls at the owner's stand: 196 before, 196 after.
