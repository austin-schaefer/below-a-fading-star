# Brief v10-A2: the full glass, and salt for news

Read `briefs/v10/_common.md` first; it is half of this brief. Port **8164**, worktree `.claude/worktrees/issue-1003-stories`.

Two acts that are beats in stories the library already holds. Read both story files whole before anything else.

**1. The twelfth chair** (`stories/the-twelfth-chair.md`; the Collation is in `src/v5/dressing/`, a table laid for twelve in a clearing in Medlar Close). The owner found the story "hard to discern from the things alone ('just a missing chair with some drag marks')". Its file has a plainer surface written and not built (beat 1: "his plate piled with food gone old, his glass full to the brim, a twelfth napkin folded. The eleven chairs all turned a little toward the gap"). Build that beat at the table, and make the act part of it: the visitor can drink to him, or fill his glass, or set something at his place; choose the one that makes a stranger understand that somebody is missing and is still being served. What answers is small and belongs to the eleven, who are never seen: decide it. A returning visitor finds their own mark at the place among the diners' (and the eleven have been back).

**2. Salt for news** (`stories/salt-for-news.md`: "partial: no turn"; the Salt-Pedlars and the split sack are in `src/v8/threads/`, the Twk-men, thumb-high riders on dragonflies who trade news for salt, in `src/v8/twk.js`). Give the story its turn through an act: at the split sack the visitor can take up a pinch of salt (or it is simply theirs to leave), and at one Twk station they can leave it. The Twk-men pay for salt with news. What they leave in return, on that visit or found there on the next, is yours: it should be a thing, small and exact, that points at another story in `stories/` (the Twk-men "are the natural witnesses to every other story here"). A carried pinch is state between two places: keep it in the acts' memory, show nothing on screen for it.

Two acts, two commits or more, one hand-back. If one is going badly, finish the other well and say so.
