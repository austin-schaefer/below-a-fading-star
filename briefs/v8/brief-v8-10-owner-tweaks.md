**#8, "Gaps at top of crystals"**: "Read as visual bugs", with a screenshot of a tall Lattice crystal whose top shows a gap (`gh issue view 8` shows the image link; the crystals are built in `src/vegetation.js`/`src/vegetation/` and the mother crystal in `src/vegetation/mother.js`; brief 01 also touched `lattice.js` with one line).

**Goal for #7.** A visitor who hops at a low wall lands on top of it and can walk along it; the same for boulders up to about chest height, the Span's parapets, the ha-ha's wall, kerbs, plinth footings, benches, the house's low walls, fallen gnomons. Nothing a person could plausibly get onto is an invisible column to the sky.

**What exists.** `ctx.addCollider({ x, z, r })` makes a cylinder of infinite height; `{ y, h }` caps it (the wilderness skill's module contract). `ctx.controls` has `addWalkable` (raise-only surfaces: brief 01's stepping slabs use it, `src/controls.js` ~285), the stairs' `walkable(x, z)`, and the Span's deck (`ctx.span.deckAt`). The hop's height is in `src/controls.js`. `tools/walk-test.mjs` and `tools/stair-test.mjs` show how the tests drive a walker; `walk-test` reports about 5,500 colliders.

**Build.**
1. Read `src/controls.js`'s collision and hop. Decide the rule once, in one place: a collider with a top (`y + h`) the walker's feet are above does not push; a top within reach of a hop can be landed on and stood on (the walker's ground becomes the top while over it); stepping off drops them. Round colliders give round tops, which is fine for rocks and posts; for walls (long thin things made of many small round colliders today, or none) add a capped segment or box collider type if the round ones make walking a wall-top lumpy, and convert the walls to it.
2. Sweep the collider sites and give a true height to everything under about 2.2 m: `src/rocks.js` (pebbles need none; stones and boulders take their mesh height), the Lynchets' walls (`src/v4/regions/lynchets.js`), the Demesne's ha-ha wall and fallen blocks, the causeway's kerbs and broken gateposts, the Plinth Road's footings and steps, the Span's parapets and the fallen bays' blocks (`src/v4/span*`: the parapets are the "railings on the edge of the big bridge"; standing on one should be possible and frightening, and falling off it must behave like falling through the crack does today), the house's benches, low walls and tables (`src/structures/taverna.js`, `src/v8/house*`), gnomons that are fallen or sunk (`src/v8/roads*`), the Collation's table, graves' edges. Tall things (trees, standing gnomons, pillars, the keep) stay as they are.
3. Keep every figure and prop that must not be stood on as it is (people, the painter's easels, lamps).
4. `tools/hop-test.mjs` in the suite: at six sites (a Lynchets wall, a boulder, the Span's parapet, a kerb, a house bench, a fallen gnomon) a running hop lands on top, the walker can take three steps along it where it is long, and stepping off returns them to the ground; walking into each without hopping still stops them. `walk-test`, `stair-test`, `roads-test`, `touch-test` (the hop ring) stay green. No measurable cost per frame: the collider count is in the thousands, so keep the test in the broad phase as cheap as it is.
